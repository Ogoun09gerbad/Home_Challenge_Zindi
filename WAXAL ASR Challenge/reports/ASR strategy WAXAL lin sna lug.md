# Reaching 0.77 through ensembling, decoding and normalization

To aim for ~0.77 in five days on an 8 GB RTX 4070 Laptop, the goal is not to train a better model than the ones that already exist. The plan is to **start from the public w2v-BERT 2.0 checkpoints built for the original WAXAL challenge**, add **per-language tuned KenLM decoding**, **orthographic post-normalization** (casing, punctuation, Lingala/Shona word boundaries) and an **MBR/ROVER selection ensemble**. The most useful reference figure comes from a participant in the original challenge: a single w2v-BERT 2.0 with a raw vocabulary (uppercase and punctuation kept) scores **0.742 on the public leaderboard (WER 0.400, CER 0.116)**, and its 26-member ROVER+MBR ensemble reaches **0.767 public and 0.7726 private** ([anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)). With the formula score = 1 − (WER+CER)/2, 0.77 therefore corresponds to the top of the original leaderboard. It implies an average error of 0.23, for example WER ≈ 0.34 and CER ≈ 0.12. The final test (Phase 2) of the original competition had **892 clips, Lingala and Shona only, without metadata** ([Team Pasketti](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place)). Effort should therefore focus on lin+sna and include language identification. One major caveat applies: **no public page for the "October Study Jam" was found**. Everything below relies on the original challenge (June 26 – August 9, 2026), and the test, languages, metric and rules must be checked today on the Study Jam's Zindi page.

On the implementation side, the foundation is already in place, which frees the five days for modeling:
- environment: Python 3.12 venv, torch 2.11+cu128, transformers 5.20;
- language model: Python `kenlm` compiled with MSVC (without `lmplz`), replaced by the pure-Python Kneser-Ney ARPA generator `build_lm.py`;
- data and training: WaxalNLP audio cached as int16 16 kHz, and multilingual CTC `train.py` (w2v-BERT 2.0 / MMS / XLS-R);
- measured training hours: lin 72 h, lug 37 h (sna in progress).

## The score reads "higher = better" and penalizes casing and punctuation

The Zindi page defines the score as the **weighted average 0.5×WER + 0.5×CER** without saying which direction is better or how text is normalized ([Zindi](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge)). Two independent participants describe a score of **1 − (WER+CER)/2, higher = better** ([Lord-LLM](https://github.com/Lord-LLM/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge) ; [anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)). This is the only reading consistent with a 0.77 target. The official starter notebook (Gemma-3n + LoRA) gets WER 52.4% and CER 14.4% on Shona ([notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Lord-LLM/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge/main/Waxal_Challenge_Starter_Code.ipynb)), i.e. about 0.67 with this formula. A control submission (`SampleSubmission.csv`) on day one is enough to confirm the direction.

The scorer's normalization is the cheapest lever. Experiments on the anyantudre model card indicate that **WER is computed on lowercased text but with punctuation, and CER on the raw string**. Moving from a cleaned 81-symbol vocabulary to a raw 97-symbol vocabulary (uppercase and `! , . : ; ?`) yielded **+0.017**, "the largest single modeling gain" ([anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)). The winning team points the same way: its post-processing deliberately adds **an initial capital letter and final punctuation** ([Pasketti postprocess](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/tree/main/postprocess)). For the same reason, keystats publishes a set of "-best" KenLMs trained on raw text with casing+punctuation ([keystats](https://huggingface.co/datasets/keystats/waxal-kenlm-models)). The official notebook, for its part, lowercases but does not strip punctuation. In practice, **never submit lowercased text without punctuation**.

Format constraints are strict. The submission is an `ID,Target` CSV, and the winning script rejects a different schema, missing or duplicated IDs, or an empty `Target` ([Pasketti](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/tree/main/postprocess)). So always write at least one character per clip. The original challenge allowed **5 submissions per day**, and 2 were chosen for the final ranking ([Zindi](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge)). The public share of the leaderboard is contradictory on the page (30% or 20%), which is fewer than 270 clips. Unsourced estimate: on such a small sample, a gap below roughly 0.005 between two submissions is noise.

The final ranking relied on a **Phase 2** test: new audio, absent from all WAXAL splits, released one week before the deadline **without language, speaker or gender** ([Zindi data](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge/data)). According to the winning team and the anyantudre card ("corrected Phase 2 test set contains no Luganda"), it was roughly half Lingala and half Shona ([Pasketti](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place) ; [anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)). Google also presents the results as covering these two languages ([Google Africa](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/meet-the-winners-of-the-waxal-speech-recognition-challenge/)). The winners combined several levers:
- **1st place (Pasketti)**: 8 systems (MMS, Granite, Parakeet, Qwen3-ASR, wav2vec, Whisper, Whisper SALT noisy-student, Sunbird Whisper-51), a weighted character-median ensemble, then orthographic rules and voting-based OOV token repair. Everything ran on a 96 GB A6000 ([Pasketti](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place)).
- **2nd place**: several analyses of the same clip, then selection of the most reliable transcription.
- **3rd place**: a longer context for Lingala, whose words "merge" ([Google Africa](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/meet-the-winners-of-the-waxal-speech-recognition-challenge/)).

No numerical scores for the winners were published. We only know that "the top three were very close". Audio denoising was "probably useless" because the test was not noisy ([Pasketti](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place)).

## WAXAL is spontaneous speech, with shared speakers, under CC-BY-SA

WAXAL ASR is not read speech. Speakers **describe Open Images pictures** (at least 50 themes, at least 15 s per recording) in their natural environment. This produces disfluencies and **code-switching**: French in Lingala, English in Shona and Luganda ([arXiv 2602.02734](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02734v3) ; [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/waxal-a-large-scale-open-resource-for-african-language-speech-technology/)). About 10% of the audio was transcribed by **a single local annotator paid per recording**, in the local script. Quality control checked clarity, language and relevance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02734v3)). **No public transcription guide** (casing, punctuation, digits, noise tags) was found ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP)). Conventions must therefore be inferred from the training transcriptions themselves: frequency of initial capitals, final punctuation, digits vs. words. Since each clip has only one annotator, spelling varies, which inflates WER much more than CER.

The `google/WaxalNLP` dataset (v2.0.0, March 2026) exposes the fields `id`, `speaker_id`, `audio` (16 kHz), `transcription`, `language` and `gender`, with an **80/10/10** split and a separate `unlabeled` split ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP)). According to WAXAL-NET, train contains **lin 71.9 h (14,400 utterances), sna 79.7 h (14,109), lug 37.3 h (5,455)**, and test lin 9.3 h, sna 9.9 h, lug 4.3 h ([WAXAL-NET](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02375v1)). These figures match the 72 h and 37 h already measured locally, confirming that the cache corresponds to the v1 split. Clips last **20.2 s** on average ([anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)), and up to about 66 s in the preview ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP)). This matters for GPU memory (see below).

The most important point for validation is speaker overlap. In v1, **up to 100% of evaluation speakers also appear in train**, and 1.09% of evaluation utterances have an exact textual twin in train. Lingala, Shona and Luganda are **not** among the languages already speaker-disjoint in v1. A re-split `data/ASR_v2/` with no shared speakers exists ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP)). Consequence: v1 validation is optimistic relative to a Phase 2 test with new speakers. You must build your own **speaker-disjoint dev from v1 train**, and not use ASR_v2 (see the compliance section).

Licenses are more restrictive than Google's blog says. The per-provider table on the HF card lists **CC-BY-SA-4.0** for lin and sna (Digital Umuganda, "AfriVoice") and for lug (Makerere, "Yogera"), whereas the blog mentions CC-BY-4.0 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP) ; [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/waxal-a-large-scale-open-resource-for-african-language-speech-technology/)). The card, being more precise, is authoritative. The share-alike clause mainly targets redistribution of derived data, but it should be mentioned in the documentation.

A fine-tuned 300M model beats every zero-shot model from 1 to 7 billion parameters ([WAXAL-NET](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02375v1)). Lingala remains the hardest language on WAXAL (~30% WER at best), even though it is easy on FLEURS. Since it makes up half of the test, every Lingala gain counts double compared to the absent Luganda. The table below gathers published figures. Three conclusions follow. First, the anyantudre family is trained on exactly the right task, with the right normalization and MUSAN+RIR augmentation. Second, Sunbird-51 brings a different architecture (encoder-decoder), which helps the ensemble. It requires forcing the `lin`/`sna` token at decoding time, hence prior language identification ([Sunbird](https://huggingface.co/Sunbird/asr-whisper-51-african-languages)). Finally, Omnilingual has the best independent figures but depends on fairseq2, whose native Windows support is unverified. The MMS and Seamless v2 models are under CC-BY-NC-4.0, and Seamless does not even cover Lingala ([Seamless](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large)). They are excluded by the "open source" constraint.

## On 8 GB, in-house training is primarily a measurement instrument

The reference recipes are calibrated for GPUs much larger than 8 GB:
- **anyantudre**: 12 epochs, LR 1e-4, batch 12 × accumulation 3, bf16, gradient checkpointing, on a 32 GB RTX 5090 ([anyantudre](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw));
- **keystats**: LR 3e-5, effective batch 32 (4 × 8), fp16, checkpointing ([keystats](https://huggingface.co/keystats/w2v-bert-2.0-multilingual-waxal-shona-lingala-luganda-best));
- **HF w2v-BERT blog**: LR 5e-5, warmup 500, dropouts and masking set to 0, `add_adapter=True`, on a 16 GB V100 ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/fine-tune-w2v2-bert)).

The blog also suggests a 5 to 15% warmup and an AdamW β2 between 0.95 and 0.98. Unmeasured estimate: with standard AdamW state, the 600M model needs about 9.6 GB just for weights, gradients and optimizer. On 8 GB, several settings must therefore be combined:
- `optim="adamw_bnb_8bit"` (bitsandbytes ≥ 0.43 has official Windows support ([bnb](https://huggingface.co/docs/bitsandbytes/installation)));
- bf16 rather than fp16 on Ada, to avoid CTC loss NaNs;
- gradient checkpointing and `attn_implementation="sdpa"` (supported for wav2vec2-bert since transformers 5.16 ([v5.16 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/huggingface/transformers/release/v5.16.0)));
- if needed, freezing the lower 12 conformer layers.

Clips average 20 s. Capping at 20 s would thus discard half the data. It is better to batch by total duration per step (batch 1–2 × accumulation 16), and only drop clips longer than ~35 s. This value is an estimate, to be validated with a 50-step trial. Under transformers v5, `warmup_ratio` is removed: pass a float `warmup_steps` instead. `eval_strategy` and `processing_class` replace the old names ([v5 migration guide](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/MIGRATION_GUIDE_V5.md)). Also keep `save_total_limit` ≥ 5 to be able to average the last checkpoints.

At the estimated pace of several hours per epoch, about 150 h of lin+sna allows 3 to 4 epochs in five days, far from anyantudre's 12. The in-house model will therefore probably not beat the public checkpoint. It remains essential for another reason: **it is the only model whose data we control**. Trained on v1 lin+sna train minus about 5% of speakers, it provides a clean dev set. The public checkpoints have seen train+val, so that dev set would be contaminated for them. This dev set is used to tune LM weights, evaluate orthographic rules, calibrate language identification and weight the ensemble. The in-house model is also an additional ensemble member. Luganda (37 h) should only be added if the Study Jam confirms it is in the test.

## KenLM helps Shona a lot and should stay optional for Lingala

The literature is unambiguous about the asymmetry between languages. In the Makerere benchmark, a Kneser-Ney 5-gram KenLM via pyctcdecode takes w2v-BERT 2.0 at 50 h **from 23.54 to 8.61 WER in Shona** (−63% relative). It only gains **about 6% in Lingala** (24.65 → 23.13), and **degrades XLS-R in Lingala** from 19.85 to 26.55 ([arXiv 2512.10968](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10968v1)). The authors attribute this to the match between text and audio. In Lingala, unstable word boundaries, echoed in the Pasketti rules, make a word LM fragile. The practical rule is to **tune alpha/beta per language on the clean dev set and keep "no LM" as a candidate for Lingala**. Suggested grid: alpha ∈ [0.2 ; 1.5], beta ∈ [0 ; 3], beam 50–100 for the search then 100–200 for the final run. pyctcdecode defaults are alpha 0.5, beta 1.5 and beam 100 ([pyctcdecode](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kensho-technologies/pyctcdecode/main/pyctcdecode/constants.py)).

On Windows, the existing pipeline works:
- **Loading an ARPA**: Python `kenlm` loads an ARPA directly. Loading is slower than a binary, but `build_binary` is not needed.
- **Adding `</s>`**: the ARPA produced by `build_lm.py` must contain the `</s>` unigram (copy of the `<s>` logprob, with `ngram 1=` incremented), which pyctcdecode requires ([HF blog n-gram](https://huggingface.co/blog/wav2vec2-with-ngram)).
- **Avoiding `fork`**: `fork` does not exist on Windows, so decode with `pool=None` or a `spawn` pool ([pyctcdecode](https://github.com/kensho-technologies/pyctcdecode)).
- **Shortcut**: keystats' `build_binary trie` binaries, including the "-best" raw-text variant, load with the already compiled `kenlm` ([keystats](https://huggingface.co/datasets/keystats/waxal-kenlm-models)).

With a raw CTC vocabulary (casing and punctuation), the LM must be trained on text **in the same raw normalization**. Otherwise the decoder penalizes every capitalized word or word followed by a period.

For the LM corpus, the WAXAL training transcriptions (excluding dev speakers) are the in-domain core, since the image prompts repeat. A filtered subset of the "clean" split of **MADLAD-400** can be added (CC-BY-4.0: sna 31.6M tokens, lin 3.4M, lug 6.1M) ([MADLAD-400](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/MADLAD-400)). Estimate: a pure-Python Kneser-Ney generator handles a few million words well. For full Shona, a 4-gram with frequency cutoffs on higher orders, or a sample, is preferable. Alternatives without KenLM (pyctcdecode's `AbstractLanguageModel` interface, flashlight decoder) are pointless since `kenlm` already imports. torchaudio's CUDA decoder has been deprecated since 2.8 ([torchaudio](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.9.0/generated/torchaudio.models.decoder.cuda_ctc_decoder.html)).

## Language identification is mainly for routing, not for acoustic decoding

A multilingual CTC model with a shared vocabulary does not need to know the language to produce a transcription. Language identification (LID) becomes essential downstream, for four uses: choosing the KenLM, applying each language's orthographic rules, forcing Sunbird-51's token and, if used, choosing the lin or sna specialist model. MMS shows that dense models lose a little against language-specific parameters (FLEURS-54: 20.7 vs. 19.1 WER at 61 languages), but that gain assumes an oracle language ([MMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13516)). With two orthographically very distinct languages, a shared model followed by LID routing is the right trade-off.

Three options exist, from cleanest to most expensive:
1. **Text LID** on the first-pass transcription. A character n-gram classifier trained on WAXAL transcriptions exploits highly distinctive markers: *zv, sv, dzv, mh, bh* in Shona; *na, ya, ko, ezali, mpe* in Lingala. It can be trained on CPU in minutes. GlotLID (Apache 2.0) serves as a cross-check ([GlotLID](https://huggingface.co/cis-lmu/glotlid)).
2. **Acoustic LID**. `facebook/mms-lid-126` includes lin, sna and lug and reaches 97.5% on 102 FLEURS languages. The argmax is restricted to the two or three relevant logits. Its accuracy drops to 80–84% out of domain ([mms-lid-126](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-lid-126) ; [MMS Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13516)). MMS weights are generally under an NC license, to be checked on the card.
3. **Lightweight classifier** on the mean-pooled encoder states of the already fine-tuned w2v-BERT. It carries no additional license. SpeechBrain's VoxLingua107 ECAPA does not cover Luganda ([SpeechBrain](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa)).

The recommendation is to combine text LID and the embedding classifier, measure their agreement on the clean dev set, and send disagreeing clips to the multilingual model without LM. Whisper should be avoided: 64.5% LID accuracy on FLEURS and no Luganda token ([MMS Table 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13516) ; [Whisper tokenizer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/whisper/main/whisper/tokenizer.py)).

## External data adds little in five days and raises duplicate issues

There is a lot of openly licensed external audio:
- **Lingala**: 110.7 h AfriVoice+LRSC+FLEURS aggregate (CC-BY-4.0 for the aggregation, upstream under access conditions) ([Lingala_100hrs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/KasuleTrevor/Lingala_100hrs));
- **Shona**: AfriVoice mirror of about 91 h, figures declared by the uploader ([shona1](https://huggingface.co/datasets/realtime-speech/shona1));
- **FLEURS**: lin, sna and lug, about 15 h each ([FLEURS](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/fleurs));
- **Luganda**: Common Voice (CC0) and Sunbird SALT (CC-BY-SA-4.0, gated access) ([SALT](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Sunbird/salt)).

Two reasons argue against integrating them now. First, there is not enough GPU time to retrain on 300 h or more. Second, **Digital Umuganda (AfriVoice) is also the provider of WAXAL lin/sna**, and these mirrors may contain clips from the WAXAL validation and test splits. Any use requires deduplication by text and by audio fingerprint. The anyantudre checkpoints have already absorbed these corpora: their `waxal-linsna` build includes the AfriVoice, FLEURS, Shunya Labs and ASR-Africa mirrors ([waxal-linsna](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anyantudre/waxal-linsna)). By reusing these models, we benefit from this data without paying the training cost. External text, on the other hand, pays off for the LM: MADLAD-400 clean, and Lingala Bible text under CC-BY-SA-4.0 ([eBible](https://ebible.org/lin/copyright.htm)). Two corpora must be excluded: Makerere Radio (CC BY-NC-ND, restricted access) ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/record/5855017)) and the IADH Lingala-TTS (research only).

If the Study Jam includes Luganda, add a three-class LID and use the Luganda outputs of the anyantudre `linsnalug` model. Luganda LM weights must be tuned separately: the LM helps there at all data sizes ([arXiv 2512.10968](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10968v1)). If the test is entirely new audio, the plan still holds, but the public checkpoint's 0.742 figure is no longer a guaranteed reference point.

## Compliance risks to address before the first submission

**Phase 1 test labels.** The test split transcriptions of `google/WaxalNLP` are public. Using them (training, LM, tuning, selection) is an explicit violation punishable by disqualification ([Zindi data](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge/data)). A less visible trap concerns **the ASR_v2 re-split**. It redistributes the whole labeled pool to remove speaker overlap, which reached up to 100% in v1 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/WaxalNLP)). It therefore necessarily moves v1 test utterances into its train. This is an inference, but it is enough to rule out training on ASR_v2 train: stick to v1 train (and v1 validation). If the Study Jam reuses the Phase 1 test, the leaderboard will be polluted by other participants anyway.

**Community models possibly trained on the test.** Sunbird-51 was trained on "WAXAL" without the splits being specified ([Sunbird](https://huggingface.co/Sunbird/asr-whisper-51-african-languages)). The keystats KenLMs exclude WAXAL validation and test, but include potentially overlapping AfriVoice mirrors ([keystats](https://huggingface.co/datasets/keystats/waxal-kenlm-models)). The original rule allowed pre-trained models "openly available to everyone" ([Zindi](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge)), and the winning team used Sunbird-51 without being disqualified. This precedent is reassuring for Phase 2. The risk is more serious if the **Study Jam reuses the original Phase 2 audio**: some public models were adapted to that audio. `yehoshua01/waxal-whisper-turbo-lin-r2` states that it used transductive pseudo-labels on the Phase 2 test audio ([card](https://huggingface.co/yehoshua01/waxal-whisper-turbo-lin-r2)). It must be excluded, and every retained card, anyantudre included, must be checked for absence of self-training on test audio.

**NC licenses.** MMS (mms-1b-all, mms-300m and their WAXAL derivatives), Seamless v2 and Sunbird `asr-mms-salt` are under CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([mms-1b-all](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all) ; [Seamless](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large)). Zindi might tolerate them as "openly available", but they violate the "open source" constraint you set for yourself. Better to stick to Apache 2.0, MIT and CC-BY(-SA). The MIT license of `facebook/w2v-bert-2.0` should be reconfirmed on its card. Finally, all external data and models must be **declared** in the final documentation ([Zindi data](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge/data)).

## Conclusion

The original competition shows that the last step between 0.74 and 0.77 does not come from compute. It comes from **alignment with the scorer** (casing and punctuation, word boundaries) and **hypothesis diversity**. Both levers cost almost nothing in GPU. The real risk is therefore not technical: it is misunderstanding the Study Jam (reused test or not, presence of Luganda) and inheriting, through community models, contamination by test labels or test audio. One hour of checking on day one is worth more than one training epoch.

The in-house model changes status. It is no longer the main candidate; it is **the clean yardstick** that makes it possible to tune, without cheating, everything grafted onto the public checkpoints: LM, rules, LID and ensemble weights. Without it, the only judges would be a public leaderboard of fewer than 270 clips, too noisy to separate gaps of a few thousandths, or the forbidden Phase 1 test labels.
