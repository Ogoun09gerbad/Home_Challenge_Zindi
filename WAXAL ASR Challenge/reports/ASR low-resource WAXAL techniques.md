# Vote and re-spell to aim for 0.76

With 48 hours left before the close, the most likely remaining gain will come neither from a new model nor from a language model. It will come from two techniques the project has not tried yet, both of which need only CPU. The first is **character-level ROVER voting anchored on a pivot member**, applied to the five clean checkpoints and their blank-penalty re-decodes, which are already cached. The second is the set of **word-boundary rules from the winning team**, whose direction was measured on the leaderboard on this same 892-clip test. For anyantudre (2nd), the blank-penalty members brought **+0.0104** in the vote and character ROVER +0.006 to +0.010 ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). For Pasketti (1st), splitting *nazomona* alone was worth +0.00227, and joining Shona progressives instead of splitting them cost 0.008 ([residual_confirmed_rules.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/residual_confirmed_rules.py); [majority_fix.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/majority_fix.py)). The project's best clean submission (**0.7521 public, rank ≈ 7**) sits exactly where anyantudre was before adding blank-penalty members (0.7512 with four members). The word deficit diagnosed in Shona (1.10 words/s versus 1.15 expected) is precisely what this vote repairs and what MBR selection cannot repair. Unmeasured estimate: **+0.005 to +0.015 cumulative**, which puts rank 4 (0.7601) within reach without guaranteeing it. The rest of the state of the art, i.e. KenLM, acoustic LID, pseudo-labeling, augmentation and new Whisper or Omnilingual members, has already been measured neutral or negative in the project, is forbidden by the compliance rules, or is out of reach of an 8 GB GPU under Windows in two days. Luganda is absent from the test (447 Lingala clips, 445 Shona) and is therefore worth nothing to the score. The public split contains only about 268 clips, with noise of ±0.0027. The ten remaining submissions must therefore stack low-risk changes, not test micro-variants.

## The test is the original Phase 2, and the remaining gap is about combination

The Study Jam Phase 2 set has **892 clips**, which the project's text LID splits into 447 Lingala and 445 Shona ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). That is exactly the size and composition of the corrected Phase 2 test of the original challenge, which "does not contain Luganda" ([linsnalug-raw card](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)). The scores also agree. soup5 alone scored 0.746054 on the original public leaderboard ([soup5 card](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsna-soup5)), and the clean ensemble F, built in part on soup5, scores 0.751052 on the Study Jam public leaderboard. The strongest inference is therefore that the Study Jam reuses the original Phase 2 audio, with two consequences. First, everything the top teams measured on the original leaderboard transfers as is, including their orthographic choices. Second, the contamination risk is concrete: `linsna-distilled` was trained on these exact 892 clips ([distilled card](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsna-distilled)), which confirms its exclusion after the fact.

The best clean submission (0.752102) is 0.008 behind rank 4 (0.7601) ([SUBMISSIONS](../SUBMISSIONS.md)). In the original challenge, 1st place finished at **0.769614 public / 0.780944 private** and 2nd at **0.766791 / 0.772552** ([Pasketti docs](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/docs/README.md); [SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). anyantudre's public history pinpoints the gap. Their four-member ensemble scored **0.751181**, the project's current level, and seven members brought it to 0.754847. The first blank-penalty member jumped it straight to **0.759338**, and seventeen members reached 0.764476 ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). Their Phase-2-audio-free recipe (`p2n_ens_bp25`, 0.764759) is nevertheless not reproducible here. It relies on seeds 43/44 and on Whisper-turbo, trained on the Phase 1 test gold, and on Sunbird-51, whose provenance is uncertain ([ensembles.yaml](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/configs/ensembles.yaml)).

## Anchored character voting adds the words MBR cannot recover

The project's MBR selection keeps, for each clip, the most central existing hypothesis. It can only add a missing word if a whole hypothesis contains it and stays central. High-blank-penalty re-decodes over-generate, so they are almost never central. anyantudre's ROVER works differently. It takes a pivot member's string as the skeleton and aligns every other hypothesis to that skeleton, character by character, with `rapidfuzz.distance.Levenshtein.opcodes`. It then sums the member weights for each position and each insertion slot, and only replaces the pivot's content if the candidate differs and totals at least **2.0** ([ensemble.py](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/waxal_asr/ensemble.py)). Two agreeing members are therefore enough to insert a word, while an isolated insertion is rejected. The author sums it up: "a vote can drop a spurious word, never recover a missing one". Hence the value of members that over-generate. Their penalties (1.0, 1.5 and 2.0) were not tuned on the leaderboard. They align the output word rate with that of the references: 1.41 words/s, versus 1.30 with greedy decoding ([INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/INFERENCE.md)).

This mechanism addresses exactly the symptom measured in the project. In Shona, the zero-penalty Phase 2 outputs drop to **1.09–1.10 words/s**, whereas the train and validation references are at 1.15. Files N and M, which raised this rate through selection, gained only +0.001 ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). The deficit exists, but selection cannot fill it word by word. Tuning a penalty from the output word rate on the Phase 2 audio uses neither labels nor training: it is a lightweight transductive decoding choice, already practiced in the project.

All the material is already on disk. The Phase 2 and validation logits of the five clean members, ft1 and keystats are in `logits/`, and the Phase 2 re-decodes at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 in `logs/v_*_phase2.csv`. Only the validation re-decodes at 1.5 and 2.0 are missing, which `make_variants.py` produces in a few minutes on CPU. The attention-mask bug that cost anyantudre 0.0016 does not affect the project: `predict.py` already passes `attention_mask` during batched inference.

The starting configuration uses **greedy soup5 as the pivot**, since it is the best single clean member on the original public leaderboard. The members are {linsnalug-raw, lin-specialist, sna-specialist, afrivoicemix, soup5} × {0; 1.0; 1.5; 2.0}, at weight 1, with a threshold of 2.0. `collapse_loops` is applied to each member before the vote, then `cap_after_stop` and the initial capital are applied to the output; both functions already exist in `postproc.py`. On validation, only three things should vary. First the threshold (2, 3 or 4), since the project has fewer members than anyantudre's 26. Then the skeleton, pivot or MBR medoid: the medoid was better on holdout (error −0.0090 versus −0.0076) but judged riskier ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). Finally the routing: all members vote on all clips, as at anyantudre, or per-language ensembles, as in F.

The medoid deserves a real trial here. anyantudre measured **−0.0014** for members from the same lineage as the pivot ([ensemble.py](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/waxal_asr/ensemble.py)). Yet the five clean members all descend from linsnalug-raw, whereas their pivot (seed 43) was a distinct lineage.

The uncertainty on the gain comes from this low diversity. anyantudre's +0.0104 came from penalties applied to **six distinct checkpoints**. Repeating the penalties on only two checkpoints had brought just +0.0006 ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). Hence the +0.003 to +0.010 range. Proposed go criterion: validation ≥ F + 0.003 (i.e. 0.8137). The val/public ordering has held so far (F ahead of FT on both), even though validation overestimates public by about 0.06.

## The test's orthographic conventions are settled on the leaderboard

Validation cannot judge word-boundary rules, and the project observed this: train-frequency corrections (`wordfix.py`) had a null effect, ±0.0003 ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). The reason is structural. The WAXAL train set is inconsistent: *na moni* is split in only **27%** of cases, and *vari kufamba* in 44% ([local statistics](<../research_notes/ASR strategy WAXAL calibration ensemble/postprocessing_lm_adaptation.md>)). Validation shares these annotators and conventions. Yet Pasketti states that the test references split *na moni* about **73%** of the time, the opposite of train. The team fixed the direction of each rule through leaderboard probes (`form_probe.py`) ([respace_probe.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/remote/respace_probe.py); [Pasketti docs](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/docs/README.md)). Since the test is the same, these probes apply to the project. And Pasketti's win on private (WER 0.329 versus 0.344 for anyantudre) indicates that the rules do not overfit the public part.

The rules to apply are explicit. They ignore casing and final punctuation, and keep the initial capital.

| Language | Order | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Lingala | 1 | Split *namoni* → *na moni* |
| Lingala | 2 | Split *nazomona* → *nazo mona* and *tozomona* → *tozo mona* |
| Lingala | 3 | Join *ba* + {zali, tie, lati, komi, fandi, vandi, sali, telemi, lakisi} |
| Lingala | 4 | Split *na* + {ye, nga, kati, se, yango, ba, biso} |
| Shona | — | Split after the *-ri* prefix before *ku-*: regex `(?i)^(a\|va\|i\|ri\|chi\|zvi\|dzi\|u\|pa\|ti\|ndi\|ku)ri(ku.+)$`, extended by `^(\w*ri)(ku\w{3,})$` |
| Shona | — | Listed *ne-* splits: `une/ane/ineruvara`, *newaya*, etc. |

Sources: [apply_best_postprocessing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/main/postprocess/apply_best_postprocessing.py); [residual_confirmed_rules.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/residual_confirmed_rules.py).

The gains measured on the original leaderboard are small but all point the same way. Splitting *nazomona* brought **+0.00227**, the *ba*+verb family about **+0.0012** (0.759759 → 0.761005, "8 out of 8" on the leaderboard), and the gap between splitting and joining Shona progressives reaches **0.008**, with WER rising by 0.0150 when joining ([majority_fix.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/majority_fix.py); [residual_confirmed_rules.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/residual_confirmed_rules.py)). The trigger counts give the order of magnitude over 892 lines: 208 for *namoni*, 126 for *nazomona*, 118 Shona changes. Two rules should not be reproduced. The concord + color split (*rwebhuruu*) was measured at −0.0085. The *ba* + noun splits are nearly 50/50 in train ([respace_probe.py](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/postprocess/remote/respace_probe.py)).

These rules use no test transcriptions, and the code is MIT-licensed. They do, however, import information derived from another team's leaderboard on this same test. This is a grey area, to be accepted consciously and declared in the documentation. Two precautions are necessary. First, do not reuse Pasketti's `waxal_tables`: their corpus `corpus/pool/*.waxal.txt` has undocumented provenance and could contain the Phase 1 test transcriptions. Stick to the explicit lists above, or rebuild the tables from the WAXAL train set only. Second, apply the rules to the voted output, as Pasketti did, not to each member.

The rest of the post-processing is already close to optimal. The raw-vocabulary members emit casing and punctuation, which protects the biggest lever: **83%** of Shona references contain internal periods. Stripping all punctuation from a perfect Shona transcription would cost about 0.08 of score, according to a local oracle simulation ([calibration notes](<../research_notes/ASR strategy WAXAL calibration ensemble/postprocessing_lm_adaptation.md>)). Commas should be neither added nor removed: comma restoration lost at anyantudre (0.764314 versus 0.764759). Capitalizing after a period (`cap_after_stop`) is risk-free and only affects CER, since the references have it in **98.6%** of cases versus 87.2% for the raw outputs ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). Loop collapsing, null on F, becomes useful again as soon as penalty-2.0 members vote, since they loop; that is the context in which it brought anyantudre +0.0069. Only the forced final period remains contradictory. It brought +0.004 for Pasketti, whereas a "period-corrected" ensemble lost at anyantudre (0.756972), probably because their members already emitted periods (inference). Since its effect is null on local validation, it only deserves an A/B submission if any are left.

## State-of-the-art models serve diversity, not the deadline

### CTC w2v-BERT remains the right foundation; Whisper is only worth it as a dissenting voice

No architecture dominates all three languages. In the Makerere benchmark (Deep Learning Indaba 2025), the best model without an LM is w2v-BERT 2.0 for Shona (**22.56%** WER at 100 h), XLS-R for Lingala (18.82% at 100 h) and Whisper for Luganda (20.45% at 200 h) ([arXiv 2512.10968](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10968v1)). On WAXAL specifically, fine-tuning matters more than size: a fine-tuned 300M model beats every zero-shot model from 1 to 7 billion parameters ([WAXAL-NET](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02375v1)). Zero-shot Whisper is unusable: large-v3 gets **93.0% WER in Lingala and 112.4% in Shona** on WAXAL ([WAXAL-NET](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02375v1)). Its hallucinations disappear mostly through fine-tuning. On Swivuriso, WER goes from 223% and 190% zero-shot to 13% and 12% after fine-tuning ([arXiv 2512.02201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02201)). Raw-character CTC keeps a structural advantage here: it emits the casing and punctuation that CER counts, with no risk of free-running looping generation. Switching to the raw vocabulary was in fact anyantudre's single largest gain, **+0.0171** ([linsnalug-raw card](https://huggingface.co/anyantudre/waxal-w2vbert-linsnalug-raw)).

The value of a fine-tuned seq2seq lies in its decorrelated errors. The top teams disagree on this point. Pasketti made it their thesis: eight model families, with Sunbird-51 zero-shot weighted 10 because its errors are independent ([Pasketti docs](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/docs/README.md)). anyantudre found the opposite: fresh w2v-BERT seeds were worth more than new architectures ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). yehoshua (4th) settles it: cross-family fusion cost **−0.0085** when one parent dominated by 0.0186, and it "only pays off when the parents are at parity" ([yehoshua0 README](https://github.com/yehoshua0/waxal-asr-phase2)). The project is in the unfavorable case. The tested Whisper LoRA scores 0.712 on validation, versus 0.776 and 0.845 for the CTCs, and added nothing to the MBR ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)).

If a Whisper model enters the vote anyway, three pitfalls are documented. The encoder's 30 s window truncates longer clips. `no_repeat_ngram_size=3` corrupts **29.2%** of Lingala transcriptions, because reduplication is grammatical in Bantu languages ([yehoshua0 README](https://github.com/yehoshua0/waxal-asr-phase2)). And greedy decoding is costly: beam 8 brought +0.0224 on anyantudre's Whisper arm ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)), and going from beam 1 to 5 +0.007 for Pasketti ([student README](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/model_whisper_salt_student/README.md)).

### Omnilingual and MMS have the best numbers, but not on this machine

Omnilingual ASR (Meta, November 2025) covers more than 1,600 languages, with CTC and LLM heads from 300M to 7B ([arXiv 2511.09690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09690)). Its WAXAL fine-tune `omniasr-llm-300m-waxal-all19` holds the best independent numbers: lin 29.9/12.5 and sna 21.6/3.9 in WER/CER ([card](https://huggingface.co/waxal-benchmarking/omniasr-llm-300m-waxal-all19)). Three obstacles nonetheless put it out of reach for the deadline. First the runtime: fairseq2 has "no native Windows support and no plans to add it" ([fairseq2 README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairseq2#installing-on-windows)), and the project has no WSL2 with sudo rights ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). Then the output format: the waxal-benchmarking models output lowercase text without punctuation, a profile that gave yehoshua, for every external Lingala candidate, "WER down, CER up, net negative" ([ssa-hubert card](https://huggingface.co/yehoshua01/waxal-ssa-hubert-lin)). Finally the voting value: the value measured for an omni member is tiny, +0.000253 ([omni-ctc1b-sna card](https://huggingface.co/yehoshua01/waxal-omni-ctc1b-sna)). The license itself is disputed. Meta announces Apache 2.0 for the code and models ([omnilingual-asr](https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr)), whereas yehoshua lists the checkpoints as CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([yehoshua0 README](https://github.com/yehoshua0/waxal-asr-phase2)). For MMS, the project's rule settles it: `mms-1b-all` is CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all)), even though MMS-1B was the most heavily weighted member (12) of the winning ensemble.

The new notes present `ravitejac/waxal-asr-solution` as the "winning" solution. It is not: at **0.757397 public / 0.766450 private**, it ranks below the top three (3rd place, Abdourahamane Ide Salifou, finished at 0.771866 private ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md))). Its MWER-objective omniASR-LLM-7B decoder does not fit in 8 GB. Its routing goes through `mms-lid-256`, an MMS derivative whose license is probably NC and needs checking, and its corrector is a LoRA on Gemma. Only its `w2vbert-{lin,sna}-v1` CTCs would be runnable, but a checkpoint named "TTA specialist" raises suspicion of test adaptation ([card](https://huggingface.co/ravitejac/waxal-asr-solution)).

Sunbird-51 deserves a mention. The top two used it: Pasketti with the forced Runyankole token, better on validation than lin/sna, and anyantudre with beam 8, at 0.737 alone. But it was trained on WAXAL without the splits being specified ([salt.sunbird.ai](https://salt.sunbird.ai/models/asr-whisper-51-african-languages/)). Abdoul27's zero-shot starting point, at 0.885 on validation, suggests it has seen the corpus ([AF51 card](https://huggingface.co/Abdoul27/waxal-af51-linsna-ck1200)). Until it is shown that the test split was excluded, the same reasoning applies as for the excluded seeds.

## KenLM, LID, pseudo-labeling and augmentation are already closed

The n-gram language model is the most widespread recommendation in the new notes, and the one most contradicted by measurements. The 4-gram KenLM built on train lowered Lingala (0.7446 → 0.7435 at the best alpha) and Shona. It only helped Luganda (0.8974 → 0.9039), which is absent from the test ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). anyantudre got the same verdict on the leaderboard ("every weight setting lost") and explains it: **76 to 84% of word errors are real words**, which an LM cannot detect ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)). The spectacular gains in the Makerere benchmark (Shona 23.54 → 8.61% WER) concern weaker models, evaluated lowercase without punctuation. The same paper also shows the LM degrading XLS-R in Lingala, from 19.85 to 26.55 ([arXiv 2512.10968](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10968v1)). The keystats LMs, which the new notes rank as priority #1, would mostly contribute text already seen by the checkpoints. An external Shona LM (MADLAD-400 clean, 31.6 million tokens) is the only untested variant that would contribute new text. It would have to be built with raw casing and punctuation and tuned per language: a day of work for an unknown gain.

Language identification is solved. The text LID (Naive Bayes on character n-grams) reaches 99 to 100% on validation ([JOURNAL](../JOURNAL.md)). MMS-LID would have added an NC license to check, for zero gain. anyantudre in fact used it only to force the Sunbird token and route their specialists: in their final ensembles, all members voted on all clips ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)).

Pseudo-labeling is excluded twice over. On the Phase 2 audio, it is forbidden by the project rules. That is precisely what disqualifies `distilled` and all `yehoshua01/*` ([pl2-spk card](https://huggingface.co/yehoshua01/waxal-mms-1b-lin-pl2-spk)). On the WAXAL `unlabeled` pool, it requires at least one day of GPU retraining, for a gain nobody has measured as positive. anyantudre lists self-training among the things that did not work ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)), noisy student degraded a Swiss German wav2vec2 (30.3% WER versus 26.4%) ([ISCA 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/schraner22_interspeech.html)), and the project's own LoRA lost on public, 0.7498 versus 0.7511 ([SUBMISSIONS](../SUBMISSIONS.md)). soup5 itself contains two members trained on pseudo-labels from this `unlabeled` pool. The Zindi page describes the Phase 2 audio as absent from all WAXAL splits ([Zindi data](https://zindi.world/competitions/google-waxal-asr-challenge/data)), which supports its status as a clean member, without audio-fingerprint verification.

Augmentation is a training technique, and it is already in place. The anyantudre members include it (MUSAN, RIR, Gaussian noise, gain). Pasketti judged denoising "probably useless" on the Phase 2 audio, which is clean ([Pasketti docs](https://github.com/Team-Pasketti/Google-WAXAL-ASR-Challenge-1st-Place/blob/main/docs/README.md)). Its inference-time variants failed everywhere: Silero VAD cost yehoshua −0.0035 ([yehoshua0 README](https://github.com/yehoshua0/waxal-asr-phase2)), speed TTA brought nothing to the project, and entropy-minimization TTA lost at anyantudre (0.765910). Same verdict for frame-level logit averaging, which fails because CTC spikes are misaligned between independent fine-tunes ([Kurata & Audhkhasi 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08311)), and for any additional soup, since soup5 is already the useful soup.

## Ten submissions for forty-eight hours

The plan assumes five submissions per day and a close on November 1. The thresholds account for the public noise of ±0.0027 ([SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/anyantudre/google-waxal-asr-challenge-solution/blob/main/docs/SOLUTION.md)).

| When | Work | Submission | Decision criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thu. 30, morning | Write the anchored ROVER; produce validation re-decodes at 1.5 and 2.0; threshold × skeleton × routing grid on validation | — | Validation ≥ 0.8137 |
| Thu. 30, afternoon | Best configuration G, with loops and `cap_after_stop` | S1 = G | Gain over 0.7521 ≥ 0.003 to conclude |
| Thu. 30, afternoon | Pasketti Lingala rules (explicit lists) | S2 = G + lin | Sign matters more than magnitude |
| Thu. 30, evening | Add the Shona *-ri ku-* and *ne-* rules | S3 = G + lin + sna | Same |
| Thu. 30, evening | Second-best ROVER configuration on validation, with the rules | S4 | Robustness of the threshold or skeleton choice |
| Fri. 31 | Combine the best; A/B of the final period; ft1 and keystats at weight 0.5 if validation rises; Sunbird-51 only if its provenance is demonstrated | S5 to S9 | Keep only differences > 0.0027 or consistent across trials |
| Sat. 1 | No experiments. Document in `DATASETS.md` the models, licenses, Pasketti rules and rate settings; freeze the reproducible code required of the top 10 | Choose the 2 finals before the close | — |

For the final selection, two clean submissions should be kept. The first is the best on public among the ROVER + rules combinations, provided it is consistent with validation. The second serves as a hedge: the same without the Shona rule, the strongest and most debated one, or F if the ROVER does not pass. Submission 1 (0.746912) must never be selected, because it contains `distilled` and `soup6`. Private (about 70% of the clips) will decide. Pasketti's rules have already held up there.

## Conclusion

This challenge presents a rare asymmetry. On a test already used by a previous competition, public post-mortems are worth more than any new model, but they are also the main contamination vector. The repositories that document the best ideas, anchored voting and orthographic rules, also host checkpoints trained on these exact 892 clips. The project's provenance discipline is what makes it possible to take the ideas without the weights. It must extend to the rules themselves, whose tables must be rebuilt from the train set alone.

The second lesson is methodological. Local validation is blind exactly where the end of the leaderboard is decided. It measures acoustics well, but it inherits the inconsistent conventions of the train set, whereas the test conventions can only be read on the leaderboard. The submission budget is therefore not just a check: it is the only instrument for measuring orthography. It should be spent on large-scale changes with high prior probability, rather than on fine-tuning below the noise threshold.
