## Frame-level posterior / log-prob averaging across CTC models (same vocab, same frame rate)

### Takeaway
Frame-level fusion of CTC posteriors is only sound when the models' CTC spikes fall on the same frames. Independently trained CTC models often put spikes on different frames, which breaks naive averaging (Kurata & Audhkhasi 2019). Checkpoints fine-tuned from the same w2v-BERT 2.0 root with the same vocab and adapter/stride config are the best case for alignment, but this must be checked empirically (frame-level argmax agreement) before trusting it. Use arithmetic averaging of probabilities (logsumexp of log-probs plus log-weights) as the safe default. Use output-level combination (ROVER or MBR) as the fallback, or ensemble with it.

### Cited Findings
- CTC posteriors are sparse and spiky. Two CTC models trained on the same data can place spikes at different frames. "Due to non-aligned spike timings, we cannot make better posteriors by computing their (weighted) averages," so naive posterior fusion and averaging-based distillation break down. Hybrid (frame-aligned) systems do not have this problem — [Kurata & Audhkhasi, Interspeech 2019, arXiv 1904.08311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08311)
- Their fix is "guided CTC training": train each CTC model so its spikes follow a pre-trained guiding CTC model. Models sharing the same guide have aligned spikes, which makes posterior fusion and distillation practical even across architectures. On 300 h Switchboard, a single word-CTC model distilled from several guided models improved Hub5'00 SWB/CH WER from 14.9%/24.1% to 13.7%/23.1%, with no LM and no data augmentation — [ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2019/kurata19_interspeech.html); [IBM Research page](https://research.ibm.com/publications/guiding-ctc-posterior-spike-timings-for-improved-posterior-fusion-and-knowledge-distillation). The fusion-only WER numbers were not readable from the PDF in this session.
- The same authors hold a patent on aligning CTC spike timing (US11302309B2). It notes that spike timing is generally not controlled in standard CTC training — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11302309)
- An alternative to frame fusion is **confidence-based model selection**: pick, per utterance, the output of the single most confident model. NVIDIA (Gitman et al., Interspeech 2023) showed that an ensemble of 5 monolingual models selected by confidence beat selection by a dedicated language-ID block, and that base and adapted models can be combined to do well on both domains. Only a small validation set is needed — [arXiv 2306.15824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.15824); [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/gitman23_interspeech.html)
- Intermediate-CTC "intra-ensemble" (learnable weighted sum of sub-model CTC outputs) exists as a training-time method (Kim et al., Interspeech 2023). It does not apply to post-hoc ensembling of separate checkpoints — [ISCA PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/kim23e_interspeech.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic vs geometric mean.** Suppose model A has blank ≈ 0.9 at frame t while model B has character c ≈ 0.9 at t (a spike off by one frame). The arithmetic mean gives ~0.5/0.5, so either label can still win and the character survives at t or t±1. The geometric mean (mean of log-probs) multiplies the disagreement and both labels are pushed down, so after renormalisation blank tends to dominate. That causes deletions, the main CTC-ensemble risk. Default to the **arithmetic mean** `log p_ens = logsumexp_i(log w_i + log_softmax(z_i/T))`. Try the geometric mean `softmax(Σ w_i·log_softmax(z_i))` only if frame-argmax agreement is very high (>97%). Pick by dev metric. (Reasoning, not from a source.)
- **Weights.** Start uniform. Then try weights proportional to `exp(−k·dev_score)` or a coordinate search on the dev set (step 0.1, about 2–3 passes). With 6–10 near-duplicate models, gains from tuned weights over uniform are usually small, and over-tuning on a small dev set is a risk. Use k-fold dev or a held-out split. (Inference.)
- **Temperature before averaging.** T only matters for averaging and beam/LM scoring. Greedy argmax of a single model is invariant to T. Use a single shared T ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}. T > 1 flattens over-confident spikes and makes arithmetic averaging more tolerant of ±1-frame misalignment. Per-model temperature scaling (fit T on dev NLL) is a cheap calibration step if models differ in confidence. (Inference.)
- **Pre-check for alignment (required).** (1) Confirm all checkpoints output exactly the same number of frames per clip. w2v-BERT 2.0 CTC heads with `add_adapter=True` and adapter stride 2 output roughly 40 ms frames; without an adapter, roughly 20 ms **[UNVERIFIED — check `model.config.add_adapter`, `adapter_stride`, `num_adapter_layers` on every checkpoint]**. Any checkpoint with a different config cannot be frame-fused. (2) Compute the fraction of frames where non-blank argmax positions coincide within ±0 and ±1 frame, per model pair, on the dev set. If ±0 agreement is low but ±1 is high, apply a small max-pool or smoothing over time (window 3) on non-blank probabilities before averaging, or fall back to ROVER/MBR.
- **Blank token id.** In HF CTC tokenizers the blank is usually the `[PAD]` token (`tokenizer.pad_token_id`) **[UNVERIFIED for these checkpoints — confirm on the 97-token vocab]**. All checkpoints must map the same id to the same character. Assert vocab JSON equality before fusing.
- **Memory and compute.** On 8 GB VRAM, run models sequentially in fp16/bf16 and save per-clip `log_softmax` arrays to disk as float16 (T_frames × 97). A 25 s clip at 40 ms is about 625 × 97 × 2 B ≈ 120 KB. Fuse on CPU (20 cores) afterwards. This also lets you reuse logits for blank-penalty, weight and temperature sweeps without re-running the GPU.

### Gaps
- No source found that directly compares frame-level CTC posterior averaging with ROVER for **wav2vec2/w2v-BERT checkpoints fine-tuned from the same SSL root**. Expected gains are therefore unknown, and the agreement check above should decide.
- Fusion-only numbers from Kurata 2019 (Tables) could not be extracted (PDF tooling unavailable).
- No published guidance found on arithmetic vs geometric averaging for CTC specifically. The recommendation above is reasoning, not a sourced result.

## ROVER (word vs character level), alignment for N hypotheses, confidence voting, Python tools, MBR vs ROVER

### Takeaway
Original ROVER (Fiscus 1997) cut WER from 44.9% to 39.4–39.7% (11.8–12.5% relative) by combining 5 heterogeneous systems. It works by iteratively aligning each hypothesis to a growing word transition network (WTN) and then voting with an α-weighted mix of frequency and confidence. For 6–10 similar CTC checkpoints, expect much smaller gains. In Python, the quickest route is `crowd-kit`'s ROVER (unweighted, any tokenizer, so word or character level). For a metric that is half CER, an MBR/medoid selection that uses the competition metric directly is a strong, simple baseline to compare against ROVER.

### Cited Findings
- **ROVER alignment.** Exact multi-sequence DP alignment is too expensive, so ROVER approximates it with iterated 2-D DP alignment (SCLITE engine). The first hypothesis is the base WTN, and each further hypothesis is aligned to the composite network using correct/substitution/deletion(NULL arc)/insertion rules. **Merge order affects the result** and is not optimised — [NIST ROVER paper (Fiscus 1997), SCTK doc](https://people.csail.mit.edu/joe/sctk-1.2/doc/rover/rover.htm)
- **ROVER voting.** score(w) = α·N(w,i)/Ns + (1−α)·C(w,i). This form is inferred from the text, because the formula is an image in the source. N = number of systems voting w, C = confidence, Conf(@) = confidence given to NULL arcs. α=1 means frequency only. Settings used: **Nist2 α=0.2, Conf(@)=0.8; Nist3 α=0.7, Conf(@)=0.6** — [SCTK ROVER doc](https://people.csail.mit.edu/joe/sctk-1.2/doc/rover/rover.htm)
- **ROVER reported gains (eval).** Best single system (BBN) 44.9% WER → Nist1 (frequency) 39.7% (−5.3 abs, 11.8% rel), Nist2 39.5% (12.0% rel), Nist3 39.4% (12.5% rel). On dev: Nist1 43.5% (2.2% rel), Nist2 40.7% (8.5% rel), Nist3 40.4% (9.2% rel). So **confidence weighting mattered much more on dev** than frequency-only voting — [SCTK ROVER doc](https://people.csail.mit.edu/joe/sctk-1.2/doc/rover/rover.htm)
- Ties in plain ROVER go to the first system's word in the network, and input order can change WER. A segment-level quality-estimation ranking was proposed to order and choose hypotheses — [ACL P15-1106](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/P15-1106.pdf); [Automatic Quality Estimation for ASR System Combination, arXiv 1706.07238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.07238)
- **Python: crowd-kit `ROVER(tokenizer, detokenizer, silent=True)`.** Input is a DataFrame with columns `task, worker, text`; `fit_predict` returns a Series indexed by `task`. Alignment is DP with cost 1 for insertion/deletion/mismatch, building a WTN, then **unweighted majority vote**. Ties are broken by more supporting sources, then longer token, then lexicographic order. Empty tokens are dropped. Example: `ROVER(lambda s: s.split(' '), lambda t: ' '.join(t)).fit_predict(df)` — [crowd-kit docs](https://crowd-kit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/texts/)
- Recent practice: one pipeline combines 3 ASR models with word-level ROVER-style voting, accepting a word only if ≥2 models agree and otherwise falling back to Whisper — [Sommelier, arXiv 2603.25750](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25750). A Greek–English production system breaks ROVER ties by average per-word confidence — [arXiv 2609.13498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13498). These details come from search snippets only.
- MOVER (2025) proposes combining multiple meeting-recognition systems as an alternative to ROVER — [arXiv 2508.05055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05055) (not read in detail).
- The NIST SCTK `rover` tool is C code in the SCTK distribution, not a Python package — [SCTK rover man page mirror](https://gitlia.univ-avignon.fr/estevey/ontrac-kaldi/blob/8dcb6dfcb61053371833d2bd88a844af10df0080/tools/sctk-2.4.10/doc/rover.1)

### Inferences
- **SCTK rover CLI** (from memory): `rover -h hyp1.ctm ctm -h hyp2.ctm ctm ... -o out.ctm -m {oracle|meth1|avgconf|maxconf} -a <alpha> -c <Nullconf>` **[UNVERIFIED flags]**. It needs CTM input with time stamps and confidences. On Windows it needs WSL or Cygwin. For CTC outputs you can make time stamps from frame indices × frame shift and confidences from the mean max-posterior over each word's frames. crowd-kit is easier on native Windows.
- **Word-level vs character-level ROVER for 0.5·WER + 0.5·CER.** Word-level voting keeps whole words, which is good for WER, but it cannot fix single-character errors inside a word where systems disagree in different places. Character-level voting (tokenizer=`list`, detokenizer=`''.join`, with spaces as tokens) can recover such words and directly targets CER, but it can create non-words and broken spacing. Run both on dev and keep the better one by the combined metric. A hybrid is: word-level ROVER, then for slots with no majority, character-level voting inside the slot. (Inference.)
- **Pivot and order.** Put the best dev system first, since it is the base WTN and wins ties. Order the rest by dev score. Optionally run ROVER for all K rotations of the top-3 pivots and pick by MBR. (Inference.)
- **Confidence-weighted voting (custom).** crowd-kit is unweighted. To get Nist3-style voting, write a ~100-line version: align each hypothesis to the pivot with a Levenshtein backtrace (e.g. `rapidfuzz.distance.Levenshtein.editops` or `Opcodes`), build slots, and score each candidate with α·count/N + (1−α)·mean_conf, using NULL confidence Conf(@). Start the grid at α ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0} and Conf(@) ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8} (the published values were α=0.2/0.8 and 0.7/0.6). Per-model weights can multiply the counts. (Inference, values from Fiscus.)
- **MBR / medoid selection.** For each clip, choose the hypothesis h* = argmin_h Σ_j w_j·(0.5·WER(h, h_j) + 0.5·CER(h, h_j)) over the K candidates. Candidates can include each model's greedy output, beam n-best, the fused-posterior output and the ROVER output. This never creates a new string, so it cannot add errors that are not in some system, and it optimises the exact competition metric. Use `jiwer` or `rapidfuzz` on CPU across 20 cores. For K ≤ 20 it costs K² edit distances per clip, which is negligible. (Inference.)
- **Expected gains.** ROVER's 12% relative came from 5 *different* sites' systems with uncorrelated errors. Checkpoints fine-tuned from the same w2v-BERT root have highly correlated errors, so expect clearly smaller relative gains (possibly a few percent relative). Measure on dev rather than assuming Fiscus-level gains. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No published, maintained pip "rover" package for ASR CTM files was found. Only crowd-kit (unweighted) and the C SCTK tool were confirmed. NeMo, ESPnet and icefall built-in ROVER utilities were not confirmed in this session.
- No source found reporting MBR-vs-ROVER relative gains for CTC checkpoint ensembles. Gains for character-level ROVER on agglutinative/Bantu languages are also unknown.

## Blank penalty / blank logit offset, insertion bonus, temperature, beam width without LM

### Takeaway
Subtracting a constant from the blank log-probability (blank penalty or "blank deweighting") is a cheap, well-established decoding knob that trades deletions for insertions. Reported settings are about 2.0 (Tiny Transducer), and a 0→2 sweep gave a large WER drop on a strongly blank-dominated model. For a well-trained w2v-BERT CTC model the best value is usually much smaller. Sweep b ∈ [0, 3] in steps of 0.1–0.25 on saved dev log-probs, separately per language, and re-tune after ensembling, since averaging changes blank mass.

### Cited Findings
- **Tiny Transducer** (on-device RNN-T): the blank posterior is deweighted during decoding to add cost to deletions. A larger deweighting value reduced deletions, but too large a value raised overall WER. They chose a **value of 2** — [arXiv 2101.06856](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.06856)
- **Myovox** (silent EMG speech, CTC): CTC posteriors were blank-dominated (peak ≈ 0.92) and the baseline release applied no penalty. Applying a blank penalty was the largest single lever: a **0 → 2 sweep cut validation WER from 77.6% to 60.6%** — [arXiv 2609.17548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17548). This is a very different domain with a weak model, so it does not show the gain size to expect for strong ASR.
- A patent describes the same idea, adjusting the blank output's probability weight during transducer decoding to reduce deletion errors — [USPTO 12367861](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12367861)
- Beam search and WFST decoding mainly fix substitutions, with smaller effects on insertions and deletions. That is why deletion-specific knobs such as blank penalty matter for greedy CTC — [arXiv 2211.14320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14320)
- Blank Collapse (compressing runs of blank frames before beam search) gives up to 78% faster beam decoding than ordinary beam search on LibriSpeech, with very small accuracy loss. It is a speed technique, not an accuracy one — [arXiv 2210.17017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17017)
- icefall/k2 has a training-time *delay penalty* for CTC (k2 PR 1086), which is separate from decode-time blank penalty — [SpeechBrain k2 graph_compiler doc](https://speechbrain.readthedocs.io/en/latest/API/speechbrain.k2_integration.graph_compiler.html)

### Inferences
- **Implementation (greedy):** `lp = log_softmax(logits, -1); lp[..., blank_id] -= b; ids = lp.argmax(-1)`, then collapse repeats and drop blanks. For the ensemble, apply b to the **fused** log-probs. Fusion (especially geometric) raises blank dominance, so the best b after fusion is usually higher than for single models. (Inference.)
- **Tuning protocol:** cache dev log-probs, grid b ∈ {0, 0.1, …, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0}, and score 0.5·WER + 0.5·CER. Also track counts of deletions and insertions (`jiwer.process_words` gives S/D/I). Pick b where D ≈ I or the metric is lowest, separately for Lingala and Shona. Expect a flat optimum. Values > 1.5 on a strong model usually start inserting spurious characters and doubled letters. **[UNVERIFIED typical-range claim; no source for w2v-BERT]**
- **Insertion bonus / word bonus.** Without an LM, "insertion bonus" is equivalent to a blank penalty in greedy decoding: a per-emitted-token bonus β is the same as lowering blank relative to every non-blank. With pyctcdecode + KenLM, the word-insertion bonus `beta` (default ≈ 1.5) and LM weight `alpha` (default ≈ 0.5) play this role **[UNVERIFIED defaults]**. pyctcdecode does not expose a blank penalty, so apply it to the logits before passing them to `decoder.decode(logits)`. It accepts log-probs or logits.
- **Space/punctuation calibration.** With cased, punctuated character targets, per-token offsets for space (word boundaries strongly affect WER) and punctuation may help. Example: a small bonus or penalty on the space token, b_space ∈ [−0.5, +0.5]. Check whether the metric normalises punctuation and case before scoring; if the metric is on raw text, punctuation errors count in both WER and CER. (Inference.)
- **Temperature.** For greedy single-model decoding T has no effect. It matters only (a) when averaging, (b) for confidence estimates used in ROVER voting or confidence-based selection, and (c) for beam search with an LM, where T interacts with alpha. (Inference.)
- **Beam width without LM.** Pure CTC prefix beam search with no LM usually matches greedy within noise for character CTC, because CTC frame outputs are conditionally independent. Beam only pays off with an LM (KenLM via pyctcdecode, beam_width 50–200; default `beam_width=100`, `beam_prune_logp=-10`, `token_min_logp=-5` **[UNVERIFIED defaults]**) or for producing n-best lists for MBR. (Inference based on CTC's conditional independence.)
- **Windows tooling.** pyctcdecode is pure Python and runs on Windows. The `kenlm` Python module usually needs a C++ build toolchain (MSVC) or WSL **[UNVERIFIED]**. torchaudio's flashlight-based `ctc_decoder` may not be available or maintained in recent torchaudio releases on Windows **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that gives blank-penalty values and WER/CER effects specifically for wav2vec2/w2v-BERT character CTC. The exact icefall/sherpa-onnx `blank_penalty` parameter names and defaults were not confirmed in this session (search results did not surface them).

## Test-time augmentation for CTC: speed perturbation, VAD/silence trimming, chunking with overlap

### Takeaway
No published study was found that measures speed-perturbation TTA (decoding 0.9/1.0/1.1× copies and combining) for CTC ASR. Speed perturbation is documented as a training augmentation (0.9/1.0/1.1), and it makes models more robust to speed-shifted test audio. Since speed changes the frame count, TTA outputs cannot be frame-averaged with the 1.0× pass. They must be combined at the text level (ROVER or MBR). For 18–25 s clips, chunking is unnecessary for w2v-BERT on 8 GB in fp16. Trimming only leading and trailing silence is low-risk. Removing internal silence should be validated on dev, not assumed to help.

### Cited Findings
- Speed perturbation at 0.9×/1.1× is used as **training** augmentation, with val/test left unaugmented (low-resource Garhwali ASR) — [arXiv 2608.10670](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10670)
- Models trained with Gaussian-noise and speed-perturbation augmentation were more robust on augmented test sets. HuBERT-SP (21.63% WER) and wav2vec-SP (34.22%) were best on speed-perturbed test sets in the S3PRL comparison — [arXiv 2303.00510](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00510v2)
- Speed-perturbation augmentation reduced WER by up to 2.92% absolute (9.3% relative) on UASpeech disordered speech (training-side) — reported in search results for [arXiv 2201.05562](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2201.05562) (snippet; not read in detail)
- TTA in general means applying several input transforms at inference and aggregating predictions, at extra inference cost — [Emergent Mind overview (secondary)](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/tta-model)
- The reported WAXAL top solution includes a model named "ft-7b-tta-spec" (a "TTA specialist" fine-tune), but its TTA procedure is not documented on the page — [HF ravitejac/waxal-asr-solution](https://huggingface.co/ravitejac/waxal-asr-solution)

### Inferences
- **Speed TTA recipe.** Resample-based speed change (`torchaudio.functional.resample` or `sox speed`, which changes pitch too) or tempo change (`sox tempo`, pitch preserved) at {0.9, 0.95, 1.05, 1.1}. Decode each copy with each model or with the fused model, then combine **text** outputs by MBR or ROVER together with the 1.0× outputs. Do not frame-average across speeds unless you time-warp log-probs back to the 1.0× frame grid, e.g. linear interpolation of log-probs along time; spike shapes will distort. Give the 1.0× output a higher weight, e.g. 2×. Expect small or no gains for models already trained with speed perturbation. Test on dev and keep it only if it helps. Cost: K speeds × M models GPU passes. (Inference.)
- **Silence (30–40% of clip).** CTC models handle silence by emitting blanks, so silence mainly costs compute and occasionally creates spurious insertions (noise mapped to short words or punctuation). Recommended order on dev:
  1. no trimming (baseline);
  2. trim leading/trailing silence only, e.g. `librosa.effects.trim(y, top_db=30–40)` (default top_db=60 **[UNVERIFIED default]**) or Silero VAD boundaries with padding ≥ 200 ms;
  3. Silero VAD segmentation of internal speech regions, decoding each segment separately and concatenating with spaces.
  
  Option 3 risks cutting words and losing cross-segment context. Only keep it if dev improves. (Inference.)
- **Silero VAD (pip `silero-vad`, PyTorch/ONNX, CPU-friendly, runs on Windows).** Typical parameters: `threshold=0.5`, `min_speech_duration_ms=250`, `min_silence_duration_ms=100` (raise to 300–500 for spontaneous speech with hesitations), `speech_pad_ms=30` (raise to 100–200 to avoid clipping onsets), 16 kHz input **[UNVERIFIED defaults — check `get_speech_timestamps` signature]**. webrtcvad: aggressiveness 0–3, frames of exactly 10/20/30 ms at 8/16/32/48 kHz **[UNVERIFIED]**. It is usually noisier than Silero on spontaneous speech.
- **Chunking.** w2v-BERT 2.0 (~600M params) on a 25 s clip in fp16 fits in 8 GB at batch size 1–4 **[UNVERIFIED memory estimate]**. Chunking with overlap (e.g. 20 s windows, 2–4 s overlap, merging log-probs by keeping central frames) is only needed for clips longer than about 30–40 s. For 18–25 s clips, decode whole clips. Sort by length and batch dynamically for throughput. (Inference.)
- **Punctuation and silence.** Since targets include punctuation, long pauses may correlate with commas or periods in references. Removing internal silence could therefore change punctuation output. This is another reason to validate VAD on the competition metric. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying speed-perturbation TTA gains for CTC ASR, or VAD-trimming gains for wav2vec2/w2v-BERT CTC on spontaneous speech with 30–40% silence. Silero/webrtcvad/librosa defaults were not fetched in this session.

## Checkpoint weight averaging ("model soups") of fine-tuned models from the same root

### Takeaway
Model soups (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022) average the weights of models fine-tuned from the **same pre-trained initialisation** with different hyperparameters. The "greedy soup" adds ingredients in order of held-out score and keeps each only if the held-out metric does not get worse. This gives better accuracy than the best single model at no extra inference cost, which matters on 8 GB and 20 CPUs. For ASR, checkpoint weight averaging is standard and reduces forgetting. It only works if all ingredients share the w2v-BERT 2.0 root **and** the same CTC head layout (identical 97-token vocab order). Combine it with output ensembling: soup the compatible group, then ensemble soups with the remaining models.

### Cited Findings
- Model soups average the weights of multiple models trained on the same task from the same initialisation with different hyperparameter configs, and improve accuracy without increasing inference time — [Wortsman et al. 2022, arXiv 2203.05482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.05482); [PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/wortsman22a.html); described as such in [arXiv 2210.15282](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.15282)
- **Greedy soup procedure:** sort fine-tuned models by held-out validation accuracy, then add them one at a time, keeping each only if held-out accuracy does not drop. The paper reports soups beating the best single model of a hyperparameter sweep on ImageNet. A ~90.9% ImageNet top-1 figure is reported (search snippets disagree: 90.98% BASIC in a footnote; 90.94% for ViT-G is commonly cited **[UNVERIFIED exact number]**) — [arXiv 2203.05482](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.05482); [ICML slides](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2022/Slides/16990.pdf)
- Starting the greedy process from the **strongest** ingredient works better than starting from the weakest — [On Defining Neural Averaging, arXiv 2508.14832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14832)
- **ASR evidence:** weight averaging is a simple, effective way to overcome catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning ASR models. The final model is obtained by averaging the latest or best checkpoints, and the authors attribute the benefit to wider optima and better generalisation — [arXiv 2210.15282](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.15282)

### Inferences
- **When it helps (for this setup).** Checkpoints fine-tuned from facebook/w2v-bert-2.0 with the same vocab, adapter config and CTC head, differing in LR, seed, data mix, augmentation or number of steps, are classic soup ingredients. Weight averaging is likely to fail, or need permutation alignment, for models from different SSL roots or with different vocab order or architecture. A continued fine-tune of another fine-tune (a "lineage") is usually still in the same basin and is a good ingredient. (Inference consistent with Wortsman's same-initialisation condition.)
- **Recipe.**
