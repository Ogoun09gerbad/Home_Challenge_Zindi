# Noisy ranking and exact top-k selection (Thurstone / Plackett-Luce / top-k LTR / choice-based sampling)

## Does the Thurstone model have an estimation literature directly applicable to recovering an additive score function s(x) under top-15% selection?

### Takeaway
The Thurstone model (comparisons/scores + additive Gaussian noise) is indeed the exact mathematical abstraction of our generation mechanism, and its estimation literature is rich — but it deals almost exclusively with estimating a scalar score per item from repeated comparisons/rankings (many comparisons per item), not with recovering a nonparametric regression function s(x) from a SINGLE noisy realization per item followed by top-k selection. No source found addresses this precise case.

### Cited Findings
- The Thurstone framework treats comparisons as true item scores corrupted by additive noise; the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL, pairwise) and Plackett-Luce (PL, partial rankings) models are special cases — [Heterogeneous Thurstone Preference Models, AAAI 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5860)
- Many estimation algorithms exist for the Thurstone model and its special cases (Hunter 2004, Guiver & Snelson 2009, Hajek et al. 2014, Chen & Suh 2015, Vojnovic & Yun 2016, Negahban et al. 2017), all estimating a vector of latent scores from repeated comparisons (pairwise or rankings), not a function of x from a single draw per item — [par.nsf.gov PDF (HTM)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10198062)
- TrueSkill is explicitly a Thurstonian model with a Gaussian score distribution: each game generates a latent "performance" normally distributed around the player's skill, and inference is done by message passing on a factor graph (Gaussian approximation by KL minimization) — [Wikipedia TrueSkill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrueSkill)
- No covariate/regression extension of TrueSkill (a "contextual skill rating" with s(x) as a function of features) was found in the results; an indirect lead exists (Kalman-type update with a linear term x_t^T θ_t) but it compares against a Kalman filter rather than extending Thurstone to covariates — [Simplified Kalman filter for online rating](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14012)
- The ICML 2009 paper on Bayesian inference for Plackett-Luce explicitly links PL to a Thurstonian model with Gaussian score distributions (TrueSkill-type), confirming the formal PL ↔ Gaussian-Thurstone link — [ICML 2009 Bayesian PL](https://icml.cc/imls/conferences/2009/papers/347.pdf)
- In "Active Ordinal Querying for Tuplewise Similarity Learning", Plackett-Luce noise and Gaussian noise are used as two distinct synthetic oracle models to generate rankings from a noisy embedding, but the paper does not estimate any score under these models (it is used for similarity metric learning, not for score recovery) — [arXiv 1910.04115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.04115)

### Inferences
- The Thurstone/BTL/PL/TrueSkill literature answers the question "how to estimate a scalar latent score per item from many noisy comparisons of THAT item" — an identification-by-repetition problem (several noisy observations of the same latent score). Our problem is the reverse: ONE single noisy observation per item (s(x_i)+ε_i), and we want to relate the latent score to x via a regression function, using the cross-sectional structure across individuals (each one's binary label) rather than repetition. These are two mathematically different problems despite the shared additive Gaussian noise — Thurstone theory therefore does not transfer directly without substantial adaptation.
- The fact that no "Thurstone + covariates x + nonparametric score function" extension appears in such a mature literature (TrueSkill is 20 years old, widely used and documented) is a fairly strong signal that this specific extension has not been formalized in this subfield — which rather corroborates the gap already identified.

### Gaps
- No source addresses estimating a nonparametric additive score function s(x) under a Thurstone model with a single draw per item. A future search should look specifically for "contextual TrueSkill" or "feature-based Bradley-Terry regression", but this was not confirmed here despite several angles tried.

## Does the recsys / "top-k learning to rank" field have empirically validated techniques for exact quota selection (top-15%), with quantified gains?

### Takeaway
The "top-k learning to rank" field is active and has empirically validated selection-bias correction methods (CLD, Heckman-LTR, Ovaisi et al.), but all of them address a problem different from ours: they correct bias in ESTIMATING A RANKER from clicks/labels partially observed on already-exposed items (position bias + selection bias in clicks), not recovering a continuous additive score function knowing that EXACTLY the top 15% of score+noise were labeled positive. No method found matches our exact mechanism (known fixed quota, known Gaussian noise, selection by rank not by exposure probability).

### Cited Findings
- "Correcting for Selection Bias in Learning-to-rank Systems" (Ovaisi et al., WSDM 2020) applies Heckman's two-step method to LTR; the selection bias there comes from the fact that clicked documents only reflect what was shown to the user (exposure selection, not quota selection on a score+noise) — [arXiv 2001.11358](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.11358)
- "Unbiased Top-k Learning to Rank with Causal Likelihood Decomposition" (CLD) decomposes the biased log-likelihood into a relevance term and bias terms (position bias treated as a confounder, selection bias treated as collider bias); the authors report that CLD and its pairwise neural extension outperform the baselines — [arXiv 2204.00815](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.00815v2)
- A doctoral thesis (UIC) extends this line of work with a "Heckman Probit" variant allowing nonlinear outcomes for deterministic selection bias (when nothing is known about the unobserved items); both approaches give more weight to item pairs that are infrequent in the top-k results — [UIC thesis](https://indigo.uic.edu/articles/thesis/Unbiased_and_Fair_Learning-To-Rank_Systems/25392868)
- "Top Rank Optimization in Linear Time" and "Surrogate Functions for Maximizing Precision at the Top" (Menon & Williamson in particular) optimize surrogates for performance at the top of a ranking (precision@top, PTop) — but these are problems of OPTIMIZING A RANKER given the true labels, not recovering a latent score given a label made noisy by quota selection — [arXiv 1410.1462](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.1462), [arXiv 1505.06813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.06813)
- Menon (2019, Machine Learning journal) shows that the PTop measure (fraction of positives at the top) and its surrogates have a flaw: for some noisy label distributions, the optimal solution is to predict the same trivial score for all instances; he proposes a rectification to avoid this — [The Risk of Trivial Solutions in Bipartite Top Ranking](https://mlanthology.org/mlj/2019/menon2019mlj-risk/)
- No result found specifically deals with an "exact top-k quota selection on score + known Gaussian noise" mechanism with a published quantified AUC/logloss-type gain — all methods found correct exposure/position biases in real recommender systems, where the exact bias-generating mechanism is not analytically known (unlike our Zindi challenge, where σ and the 15% quota are known/inferable quantities).

### Inferences
- The "recsys / top-k LTR" field solves an econometric observation-bias problem in real systems where the selection mechanism is unknown or must be estimated (propensities). Our problem is the reverse and in one sense simpler: the selection mechanism IS known (exact quota + Gaussian noise), so propensity-based methods (CLD, Heckman-LTR) are over-parameterized / ill-suited — they try to estimate a bias that we already know analytically in our case.
- The "precision/ranking at the top" literature (Menon, Li et al. TopPush) is close in vocabulary ("top", "implicit quota") but solves the reverse of our problem: optimizing a ranker to rank well given the true labels, whereas we want to recover the latent score given a label generated by a noisy quota-selection mechanism. It is not transferable without substantial reformulation.

### Gaps
- No recsys technique with quantified gains (e.g. AUC/logloss improvement) for our exact mechanism was found. A "noisy top-k identification" sub-literature in statistical learning theory (e.g. Chen, Fan, Ma, Wang, Annals of Statistics 2019 — "Spectral MLE") may be closer, but it concerns identifying the TOP-K ITEMS themselves (0-1 loss on the set), not regressing an underlying function s(x) — to explore if a future search is deemed useful, but outside the scope of this pass.

## Does this second search confirm or refute the literature gap already identified?

### Takeaway
The gap is CONFIRMED from these new angles as well. No source found — neither in Thurstone/Plackett-Luce/TrueSkill, nor in top-k LTR, nor in ML choice-based sampling, nor in selection-biased PU learning — builds a regression theory for recovering an additive function s(x) under EXACT quota/rank selection (top-15%) with known Gaussian noise.

### Cited Findings
- The closest lead found is PU learning with biased positives (non-SCAR): Teisseyre, Martens, Bekker & Davis (AISTATS 2025), "Learning from biased positive-unlabeled data via threshold calibration", deals precisely with the case where "the observed positive labels are a biased sample of the positive distribution" and proposes a new assumption on the labeling mechanism combined with robust statistics results, without estimating propensity scores — [PMLR v258](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/teisseyre25a.html)
- However, the full text (PDF, threshold calibration) could not be retrieved (404 on the tested PDF URL); only the abstract could be consulted, which does not specify whether the labeling mechanism is an exact quota/rank rather than an x-dependent probabilistic propensity, nor whether the latent score is assumed additive — [proceedings.mlr.press/v258/teisseyre25a](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/teisseyre25a.html)
- The standard correction approach in choice-based sampling (Manski & Lerman 1977, WESML) reweights each chosen observation by the population/sample ratio of choice shares, which is a weighting mechanism based on a known selection PROBABILITY — not on exact RANK among individuals, hence not directly applicable to relative top-k selection — [choicer WESML doc](https://fpcordeiro.r-universe.dev/choicer/doc/wesml.html)

### Inferences
- The key structural difference that seems to explain the persistent gap: almost all of the selection-bias correction literature (Heckman, WESML, PU-learning SCAR/SAR, propensity-based LTR) models selection as an individual PROBABILITY function (propensity p(x) or fixed threshold on s(x)+ε), whereas our mechanism is a RELATIVE rank among ALL observations of the same draw (exactly the top 15%, whatever the absolute noise level on that day). This "across-unit" dependence (being positive depends on the scores of the OTHER individuals, not only on one's own) falls outside the framework of independent individual-propensity selection models used in Heckman/WESML/PU-SAR, and appears to be precisely what is missing from all the literature covered in both research passes.
- The closest candidates identified at this stage (Teisseyre et al. 2025 for non-SCAR biased-selection PU learning, and Thurstone/TrueSkill theory for the Gaussian noise mechanism) each cover half of the problem (biased selection on one side, additive Gaussian noise on the other), but neither combines them with an EXACT RANK constraint (fixed quota among N individuals) AND a nonparametric score function in x.

### Gaps
- The full text of Teisseyre et al. (2025) could not be examined (no PDF access via the available tools); it remains possible (but unconfirmed) that their "new assumption on the labeling mechanism" covers a relative-rank selection case — to be checked if someone has access to the full PDF or the associated GitHub repository (PUBiasCalibration).
- No source found provides a theoretical bound or quantified empirical result (e.g. AUC/logloss gain) for our exact top-k quota selection mechanism with known Gaussian noise — which means that any method applied to the Zindi challenge would be an original construction (or an undocumented adaptation of an existing method), not the direct application of a published result.
