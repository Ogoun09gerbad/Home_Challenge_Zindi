# Recent advances (2024-2026) in gradient boosting / tree ensembles for binary tabular classification

Context taken as given (not re-researched): ~40k rows, 15 features (selected from ~350 monthly aggregates), 85/15 target, signal = smooth additive function + Gaussian noise + probit link, current pipeline LightGBM/CatBoost depth 1 + custom probit loss, bagged 10 folds × 7 seeds, blended with P-spline GAM. Estimated single-model ceiling ~0.0046 logloss above the current score.

## What recent algorithmic innovations (2024-2026) in XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, or alternatives (PGBM, NGBoost, EBM, Bayesian boosting, boosting with uncertainty) deliver a measured gain on tabular benchmarks with a rare target and additive structure?

### Takeaway
No identified advance has been measured specifically on a noisy additive signal with a rare target at a comparable size (~40k rows, 15% positives). The most solid developments in 2024-2026 concern (a) tabular foundation models (TabPFN-2.5, BoostPFN), which claim generic gains vs. tuned GBMs, and (b) rebalanced losses for GBDTs on imbalanced datasets, but measured in F1 on generally smaller datasets or with different imbalance ratios (no AUC/logloss reported). The XGBoost/LightGBM 2024-2026 changelogs are almost exclusively engineering improvements (external memory, GPU, categoricals, Arrow/Polars IO), not accuracy gains on additive signals.

### Cited Findings
- TabPFN-2.5 (arXiv 2511.08667, late 2025): tabular foundation model; the authors claim it outperforms tuned gradient boosting libraries out of the box, including on datasets with 500-2000 features. This is the authors' own claim, not independently replicated in the sources found. — [arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667), digest [alphaxiv.org/fr/overview/2511.08667](https://www.alphaxiv.org/fr/overview/2511.08667)
- TabPFN v2 (the version preceding 2.5) is natively limited to 10,000 training rows, 500 features and 10 native classes — i.e., below the competition's ~40k rows; TabPFN-2.5 is presented as the version that pushes these limits, but no precise row limit was confirmed in the sources consulted. — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TabPFN)
- MultiTab (arXiv 2505.14312, 2025): across 42 datasets, TabPFN beats ModernNCA on 29/42; its advantage is most pronounced on the least balanced datasets, leading the authors to conclude that it is particularly well suited to imbalanced classification. Figures reported via an aggregated summary, not verified against the full primary text. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14312)
- BoostPFN (Wang et al., AISTATS 2025, arXiv 2503.01256): treats a prior-fitted network (PFN) as the weak learner in a boosting loop, with residual reweighting and subsampling without replacement at each round; the authors report that it can outperform a standard PFN at an equal number of samples on large datasets, and train significantly faster than GBDT/DL/AutoML, with gains sustained up to 50× the PFN's pre-training size. Code available (github.com/yxzwang/BoostPFN). — [arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01256](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01256), [proceedings.mlr.press/v258/wang25d.html](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/wang25d.html)
- "PFN-Boost" (secondary description, not verified against a primary source): reportedly initializes a GBDT with the predictive scores of a pre-trained PFN before boosting; uncertain attribution (Jayawardhana et al., February 2025, cited only by a third-party aggregator). Treat with caution until the primary source is confirmed. — [api.emergentmind.com/topics/pfn-boost](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/pfn-boost)
- GRANDE (arXiv 2309.17130): learns axis-aligned tree ensembles via gradient descent rather than sequential boosting; the authors report that it outperforms GBMs and deep learning on the majority of 19 classification datasets. Caveat: initially published in 2023; a v3 revision is still circulating in 2025, but the method itself predates the requested 2024-2026 window. — [arxiv.org/html/2309.17130v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17130v3)
- Study on rebalanced ("class-balanced") losses for GBDTs (Luo, Yuan, Xu; Neurocomputing, June 14, 2025; arXiv version 2407.14381): tests XGBoost, LightGBM and SketchBoost (not CatBoost) with 6 loss variants (baseline CE, WCE, Focal Loss, ASL, and two new variants ACE/AWE) on 40 imbalanced datasets (15 binary, 15 multi-class, 10 multi-label). Results in F1 (no AUC or logloss reported): in the binary case, 13/15 datasets improved by 0.38% to 28.91% in F1, 2 datasets slightly degraded (us_crime −0.43%, sick_euthyroid −0.46%). The binary imbalance ratios tested range from 8.6 to 111 (thus in the same range as 85/15 ≈ IR 5.7, but the methodology covers no dataset of roughly 40,000 rows with 15% positives — the closest is "webpage" with 34,780 rows but an IR ≈33, i.e. ~3% positives, not 15%). — [arxiv.org/html/2407.14381v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14381v1)
- PKBoost (Rust crate, self-published): claims a 10-18% gain over XGBoost/LightGBM under extreme imbalance (0.2% fraud rate) and under drift. No independent or peer-reviewed evaluation found; treat as unverified. — [rust-digger.code-maven.com/crates/pkboost](https://rust-digger.code-maven.com/crates/pkboost)
- ASWBoost (PLOS ONE, 2026): modifies AdaBoost's exponential loss (not gradient boosting) via a θ parameter for noisy and imbalanced data. Adjacent method, not a GBDT. — [econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0356889](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:plo:pone00:0356889)
- XGBoost changelogs (v3.0, February 2025; v3.1, September 2025): the new features concern external memory (training on large data), a categorical re-encoder that freezes the encoding at training time for inference, SYCL support, and `QuantileDMatrix` extended to all prediction types. Nothing affecting accuracy on additive signals or rare targets. — [xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changes/v3.0.0.html](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changes/v3.0.0.html), [xgboost.readthedocs.io (v3.1)](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/stable/changes/v3.1.0.html)
- LightGBM changelogs (v4.6, March 2025; v4.7, 2026): Blackwell/ROCm GPU support, linear trees on GPU (a feature already available on CPU for years, extended to GPU), per-query bagging for LambdaRank, Polars/Arrow interoperability. No substantive algorithmic innovation for accuracy. — [newreleases.io/project/pypi/lightgbm/release/4.6.0](https://newreleases.io/project/pypi/lightgbm/release/4.6.0), [gitclear.com (v4.7.0)](https://www.gitclear.com/open_repos/Microsoft/lightgbm/release/v4.7.0)
- CatBoost: no 2024-2026 changelog could be found in the sources consulted (search results limited to 2020-2021 versions, e.g. uncertainty sampling via `virtual_ensembles_predict` and `posterior_sampling`, which predate the requested window). Explicit gap — to be re-checked directly at github.com/catboost/catboost/releases. — [newreleases.io/project/pypi/catboost](https://newreleases.io/project/pypi/catboost/release/0.24.2)

### Inferences
- The most credible recent gains for rare targets come from **rebalanced losses** (WCE/Focal/ASL/ACE/AWE) rather than from a redesign of the boosting algorithm itself — but they are measured in F1 at decision thresholds, a metric of little relevance to a logloss/AUC objective like the competition's, and no tested dataset matches the competition's size/imbalance combination.
- Tabular foundation models (TabPFN-2.5, BoostPFN) are the "newest" direction of 2024-2026, but nothing shows that they capture a pure additive function + Gaussian noise better than monotonicity-constrained depth-1 trees, which are already near-optimal; their documented advantage concerns speed, small sample sizes, or complex data structures (categoricals, interactions), not a clean additive signal.
- Overall, the "official" libraries (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) have not published any 2024-2026 innovation affecting modeling accuracy for an additive signal with a rare target; their recent efforts focus on engineering (speed, memory, data formats).

### Gaps
- No source found measures a net gain on a ~40,000-row dataset with exactly 15% positive target and pure additive structure — comparability of the cited benchmarks (class-balanced losses, MultiTab) remains approximate.
- CatBoost 2024-2026 changelog not found via web search; requires direct verification on the official GitHub repository.
- No independent replication of the TabPFN-2.5 / BoostPFN claims on rare-target datasets was found — these are results self-reported by the authors.

## Are there boosting variants designed specifically for non-logistic link functions (probit, cloglog) or for learning the link itself?

### Takeaway
No mainstream boosting library (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) natively offers a probit or cloglog objective in 2024-2026 — as indirectly confirmed by the absence of any mention found, which validates the need for the custom probit loss implementation already used in the pipeline. The closest academic lead is a boosting+tree hybrid with a probit link published just before the requested window (late 2023), and a recent (2026) extension of BART (Bayesian trees, not boosting) to multi-label, which builds on a binary probit-link BART.

### Cited Findings
- "On subbagging Boosted Probit Model Trees" (Qin & Huang, arXiv 2311.02827): introduces the Probit Model Tree (PMT) as a base learner inside an AdaBoost procedure ("ProbitBoost"), then combines it with subbagging (SBPMT), motivated by a bias-variance decomposition; tested on the Mease and Wyner simulation. **Caveat: dated November 2023, thus just outside the requested 2024-2026 window**, but it is the only probit-link boosting method found directly. — [ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.02827](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.02827)
- MLCBART (arXiv 2601.08964, 2026): extension of BART (Bayesian Additive Regression Trees — Bayesian tree ensembles, not gradient boosting) to multi-label classification, built on earlier binary probit-link BART and multiclass BART; proposes a new MCMC scheme for the mean and correlation parameters. Does not specifically address class imbalance or rare targets. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08964](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08964)
- Original BART (Chipman, George, McCulloch, 2010, predates the window but relevant as a reference): the classification version natively uses a probit link via latent data augmentation (Albert-Chib) — historically the only family of tree ensembles to expose a "native" probit link rather than one approximated by a custom loss. — [www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Projects/Trees/BayesianCART/BART.pdf](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~scs/Projects/Trees/BayesianCART/BART.pdf)
- "Verifiable Boosted Tree Ensembles" (arXiv 2402.14988, February 2024): treats the link function as a generic choice (mentions the logit as an example for turning the raw score into a probability) but neither proposes nor specifically studies a probit or cloglog link. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.14988)
- Hamura (arXiv 2410.10618, October 2024): proposes an "approximate identity" link with heavier-than-exponential tails for Bayesian GLMs — a fixed alternative link choice, not a learned link, and not in a boosting/tree framework. Limited relevance. — [arxiv.org/abs/2410.10618v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10618v1)
- Older work (pre-2024, cited for context only) on estimating the link via boosting (Tutz & Petry, Journal of Applied Statistics 2016) shows that estimating the link jointly with the effects can improve performance when the link is misspecified — but this is not a 2024-2026 publication and does not concern trees. — [epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/15266/1/tr145.pdf](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/15266/1/tr145.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is apparently no tree-boosting alternative published in 2024-2026 that natively offers a probit or cloglog link better integrated than the custom Φ(η) loss already used in the pipeline. The current approach (homemade probit loss on LightGBM/CatBoost) therefore seems to remain at the frontier of what is publicly documented.
- The BART/probit direction (Bayesian, not boosting) could in theory better exploit a probit link natively via Albert-Chib latent data augmentation, but no 2024-2026 evaluation on a noisy additive signal with a rare target was found for this model family, and BART is in any case not a boosting ensemble in the strict sense the question asks for.

### Gaps
- No empirical evaluation found comparing ProbitBoost/SBPMT or a BART-probit with a GBDT using a custom probit loss on a similar dataset (rare target, additive signal, ~40k rows).
- No confirmation that a mainstream boosting library (XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost) added a native probit/cloglog objective between 2024 and 2026 — the changelogs consulted do not mention it, but an exhaustive search of all GitHub issues was not carried out.

## Which variance-reduction techniques (bagging, distillation, feature perturbation, DART-style tree dropout) have shown a documented net gain on datasets of comparable size (tens of thousands of rows)?

### Takeaway
Few 2024-2026 innovations were found on this specific point: the recent literature consulted remains essentially theoretical (bias-variance bounds for feature subsampling) and provides no empirical measurement of net gain for GBDTs at the scale of tens of thousands of rows. CatBoost's Minimal Variance Sampling (MVS), although predating 2024, remains the most-cited reference as a row-subsampling method with a documented gain, and is still CatBoost's default setting.

### Cited Findings
- Minimal Variance Sampling (Ibragimov & Gusev, NeurIPS 2019 — predates the window but is still the current standard used by default in CatBoost): reduces the number of examples needed per boosting iteration and significantly improves model quality compared with previous state-of-the-art sampling methods. Relevant as confirmation that the pipeline's current bagging (10 folds × 7 seeds) rests on solid foundations, but not a 2024-2026 innovation. — [ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.13204](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.13204)
- 2025 theoretical study (identified only via an aggregated summary; exact title and arXiv ID not confirmed in the search results) on greedy forward selection with feature subsampling: shows, under an orthogonal design with OLS base learners, that ensembling with feature subsampling can reduce both bias and variance, not just variance as is commonly assumed. Limited scope: strong assumptions (orthogonal design, OLS), not an empirical result on GBDTs or real data. — secondary source only, to be verified (arXiv not confirmed)
- 2024 paper on "subsampled ensembles" (exact title and arXiv ID not confirmed): argues that a "tail enhancement" type gain from majority-vote subsampling goes beyond simple variance reduction, but concerns row subsampling with majority voting across models, not specifically tree dropout (DART) or boosting. — secondary source only, to be verified

### Inferences
- No evidence found that DART (tree dropout) or feature perturbation brings a documented net gain specifically on datasets of tens of thousands of rows in 2024-2026 — the only available evidence on DART is the original publication (2015) and an informal tutorial example, not a recent controlled study.
- The pipeline's current bagging (10 folds × 7 seeds) already seems to rely on the best-established variance-reduction techniques (MVS-type); no 2024-2026 innovation was identified that would suggest a clearly superior variance-reduction technique for this size regime.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 empirical study found measuring a net gain from DART, feature perturbation, or distillation specifically for GBDTs at the 10k-100k row scale with a rare target.
- The two theoretical papers mentioned (feature subsampling bias/variance, subsampled ensembles) could not be confirmed with an exact title or a reliable primary arXiv URL within the allotted time — to be located and verified before citation in a final report if this direction is deemed a priority.
- No information found on model distillation (teacher-student) applied to tabular gradient boosting to reduce variance on small datasets in 2024-2026.

## Are there recent results on refining GAMs/additive boosting (P-splines, spline boosting, neural additive models) that would beat a LightGBM+CatBoost+GAM blend on this kind of signal?

### Takeaway
The 2024-2025 work on GAMs/NAMs/additive boosting mainly brings theoretical clarifications (convergence, pathologies) and neural variants (ProtoNAM) that compare themselves to other NAMs, not directly to a GBDT+GAM blend. No source found demonstrates that any of these methods beats a depth-1 LightGBM+CatBoost + P-spline GAM ensemble on a noisy additive signal with a rare target — this is an explicit and important gap given the goal of the research.

### Cited Findings
- ProtoNAM (arXiv 2410.04723, October 2024; peer-reviewed version in ACM Transactions on Knowledge Discovery from Data, October 2025): "Prototypical Neural Additive Model", introduces prototypes into a neural-network-based GAM framework, with a hierarchical shape-function modeling method inspired by gradient boosting. The authors claim it outperforms all existing neural-network-based GAMs (self-reported comparison, not against GBDT+GAM). — [arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04723)
- ProtoNAM's related-work section cites the Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM) as an example of a GAM refined by boosting techniques, and NODE-GAM as a method built on EBM. Neither is dated 2024-2026 (EBM and NODE-GAM predate the window), but they are the two most-cited "boosting + GAM" methods as an alternative to a LightGBM+CatBoost+P-spline GAM blend. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04723)
- PRISMA systematic review (arXiv 2510.24601, 2025) comparing GAMs and neural networks on tabular data, covering 143 papers and 430 datasets: concludes that GAMs remain competitive with an interpretability and transparency advantage, and presents GAMs and NNs as complementary tools rather than a clear winner. No comparison specific to a GBDT+GAM blend is reported in the summary consulted. — [arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24601](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24601)
- "Additive Model Boosting: New Insights and Path(ologie)s" (Schulte & Rügamer, AISTATS 2025, arXiv 2503.05538): studies solution paths and convergence of componentwise boosting for additive models (P-spline-type boosting); identifies "pathologies" in the convergence behavior for certain classes of additive models that call for caution, while providing general theoretical support for the use of additive boosting in practice. According to a partial reading of the text (HTML version), P-spline boosting converges to the unpenalized B-spline solution despite a penalized fit at each step — regularization is therefore mainly controlled by the number of stopping iterations rather than by the penalty parameter. No direct quantitative comparison against a LightGBM+CatBoost+GAM blend was found in the excerpts consulted. — [arxiv.org/abs/2503.05538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05538)
- GP-NAM (2024, mentioned via a secondary search, primary source not consulted in detail): NAM variant with a convex objective whose number of parameters grows linearly with the feature dimension — presented as lightweight, but no performance figures compared against GBDT were found. — via aggregated search summary, to be verified

### Inferences
- No evidence was found that a 2024-2026 GAM/NAM/additive-boosting method specifically beats a depth-1 LightGBM+CatBoost (probit loss) + P-spline GAM blend on an additive signal + Gaussian noise with a rare target — the literature mostly compares GAMs/NAMs with each other or against deep neural networks, rarely against an already-optimized GBDT+GAM ensemble like the current pipeline.
- The most actionable finding is the caveat from Schulte & Rügamer (2025): componentwise P-spline boosting can exhibit pathological convergence behavior depending on the additive model class, and the effective regularization depends mainly on the number of stopping iterations rather than on the penalty parameter chosen a priori — this could have a practical implication for tuning the pipeline's P-spline GAM (check whether early stopping is better controlled than the penalty strength), but it is only an inference and would require a full reading of the paper before acting on it.

### Gaps
- No direct empirical comparison found between (ProtoNAM / EBM / NODE-GAM / componentwise P-spline boosting) and a LightGBM+CatBoost+GAM blend on an additive signal + Gaussian noise with a rare target of comparable size (~40k rows, 15% positives).
- The full text of "Additive Model Boosting: New Insights and Path(ologie)s" could not be extracted as readable text (binary PDF) within the allotted time; only a partial reading via a third-party HTML version was possible. A full reading of the PDF (saved locally by the fetch tool) would be needed to extract the exact numerical results and practical recommendations.
- EBM (Explainable Boosting Machine) and NODE-GAM have not been verified as having received significant updates in 2024-2026 — to be confirmed if this direction is deemed a priority.
