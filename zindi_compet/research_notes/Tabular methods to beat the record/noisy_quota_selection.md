# Estimation under exact-quota selection of a noisy latent score (top-K% of s(x)+ε)

Context taken as given (not re-researched): label = indicator of membership in the 15% of rows (exact quota by position) with the highest s(x)+ε, s(x) smooth additive, ε Gaussian (empirically confirmed against Student-t and GND). Current model: probit P(y=1|x)=Φ(s(x)-threshold) via GBM/GAM. Best local score 0.22786, rank-1 target = 0.223771.

## Is there literature on estimation under an "exact top-K% quota" mechanism (as opposed to a fixed-threshold probit), and can exploiting "exactly 15% positives" reduce variance compared with a pointwise probit likelihood?

### Takeaway
There is a mature econometric literature (Cosslett 1981/1987/2006-2007; Imbens 1992; Imbens & Lancaster 1996) that formally shows that incorporating an **exactly known** aggregate share (e.g. P(y=1) or the exact number of cases) as an additional constraint/moment yields an estimator strictly more efficient than standard MLE that ignores this information — but the documented gain mainly concerns the **threshold/intercept** (location parameter), not the shape of s(x) itself; no source found addresses the precise case of a continuous additive score with an exact quota by position (rather than by population probability).

### Cited Findings
- Cosslett (1987, *Econometrica* 55(3)) derives efficiency bounds for "distribution-free" estimators of the binary choice model and the censored model (Tobit); without a restriction on F, estimation of the intercept has no finite bound in general, but a symmetry restriction on the error yields a finite bound — adaptive estimation (no loss vs. parametric) is in general not possible for this model — [Efficiency bounds for distribution free estimators of the binary choice and the censored regression models](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1987/05/01/efficiency-bounds-distribution-free-estimators-binary-choice)
- Cosslett (2006 working paper / 2007 *International Economic Review*), "Efficient Estimation of Semiparametric Models by Smoothed Maximum Likelihood" — the smoothed-likelihood estimator explicitly uses the **known population shares** of the two choices to gain efficiency over the standard case, and attains the semiparametric bounds both for the structural parameters and for functionals of the error density — [SmoothedLikelihood.Jan2006.pdf](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/cosslett.1/papers/SmoothedLikelihood.Jan2006.pdf) (PDF not usable via automatic extraction; summary obtained via secondary search, to be verified against the primary source)
- Imbens (1992, *Econometrica* 60(5), pp. 1187-1214), "An Efficient Method of Moments Estimator for Discrete Choice Models with Choice-Based Sampling" — the proposed estimator is efficient and **can directly incorporate information on known marginal (aggregate) choice probabilities**, which simplifies the procedure compared with earlier estimators — [scholar.harvard.edu PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/imbens/files/an_efficient_method_of_moments_estimator_for_discrete_choice_models_with_choice-based_sampling.pdf) (direct fetch access denied — HTTP 403; citation and summary via secondary search)
- A secondary source (a paper on causal identification) reports that according to Imbens (1992) and Imbens & Lancaster (1996), when additional population moments (e.g. aggregate P(y=1)) are known, re-estimating these moments from the sample can further improve efficiency — [Bridging Methodologies: Angrist and Imbens' Contributions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13023)
- Imbens & Lancaster (1996, *Journal of Econometrics* 74(2)), "Efficient estimation and stratified sampling" — proposes an estimator that is efficient in the sense of Chamberlain (1987) and simpler to compute than existing alternatives (including Cosslett 1981) — [scholar.harvard.edu](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/imbens/files/efficient_estimation_and_stratified_sampling.pdf)
- The exact-quota selection mechanism ("top-K by position") corresponds, in order-statistics theory, to the notion of **concomitants of order statistics** (David & Nagaraja): when a bivariate sample is sorted by X (here the noisy score) and the k largest values are retained, the associated Y (here the covariates x) are the "concomitants" of the k largest order statistics — a theory developed for selection, ranked-set sampling, and prediction — [Concomitant (statistics), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concomitant_(statistics)); reference chapter: David & Nagaraja, *Order Statistics: Theory & Methods* (Balakrishnan & Rao eds., Elsevier, 1998), pp. 487-513, DOI 10.1016/S0169-7161(98)16020-0
- Nagaraja & David (1994, *Annals of Statistics* 22(1), pp. 478-494) derive the (exact and limiting) distribution of the maximum of the concomitants of the k largest order statistics, with k fixed or k/n fixed — a mathematical framework close to a "top-15%" selection — [cited reference](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/437276)
- The "exact quota" mechanism (fixed number of positives by construction) is structurally the analogue, in reliability/survival analysis, of **Type II censoring** (observation stops after a fixed number r of failures, as opposed to Type I censoring at a fixed time/threshold) — Kong & Fei (1996, *Annals of the Institute of Statistical Mathematics* 48(4)) establish conditions under which the MLE for multiply Type II censored samples is consistent, asymptotically normal and efficient (Weibull, gamma, logistic) — [ism.ac.jp PDF](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/pdf/048_4_0731.pdf)
- In the case of contingency tables/logistic regression with margins fixed by construction (retrospective case-control sampling), the likelihood conditional on the number of cases per stratum follows a multivariate non-central hypergeometric distribution; conditioning removes the need for a model of the margin and makes estimation valid under fixed-margin sampling — [arXiv 2005.06158, An Asymptotic Result of Conditional Logistic Regression Estimator](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.06158)
- Andersen (1973): in the Rasch model (exponential family), conditioning on the minimal sufficient statistic (the raw score) eliminates the incidental parameters and yields consistent conditional-likelihood estimators for the structural parameters — a general principle of "conditioning on an exact sufficient statistic" directly analogous to "conditioning on the fact that exactly 15% of rows are positive" — [secondary synthesis, rasch.org memo](https://www.rasch.org/memo20b.pdf)

### Inferences
- The principle "an exact, known margin constraint improves efficiency compared with an estimate that leaves it free" is well established in econometrics (Cosslett, Imbens, Imbens & Lancaster) and psychometrics (Andersen/Rasch), but in all these cases the documented gain concerns a **location/threshold parameter** (the intercept, or the incidental parameters), not the shape function s(x). Applied to the Zindi problem, this suggests that explicitly fixing the decision threshold at the exact empirical 85th percentile of the fitted score (rather than letting the probit MLE estimate it freely) could slightly reduce variance on that single parameter — but this gain is probably marginal if n is large and the probit is already well specified (most of the uncertainty comes from estimating the additive shape s(x), not the threshold).
- The mathematical framework closest to the exact mechanism described (sorting + selecting the k largest values of a noisy score, with the covariates being the "concomitants") is the theory of **concomitants of order statistics** / **Type II censoring**. However, no source found develops a regression estimation theory (recovering an additive function s(x) from the observed covariates of the selected items) built specifically on this framework with a proof of efficiency gain over the standard probit likelihood.
- Conditioning exactly on "15% of the n rows are positive" (rather than on a population probability) is structurally closer to Andersen's conditional likelihood (an exact sufficient statistic, fixed by construction) than to the Cosslett/Imbens frameworks (which concern a population probability, not an exact count in the observed sample). But the precise construction of an analogous conditional likelihood for a probit model with continuous Gaussian noise was not found in the literature consulted — this is probably a real gap rather than a search oversight, since the Rasch/case-control setting is a discrete exponential-family framework, not a continuous Gaussian threshold model.

### Gaps
- No primary source consulted in full text for Cosslett (2006/2007) or Imbens (1992) — the Cosslett PDF could not be extracted (raw binary content), and the Imbens PDF returned HTTP 403. The summaries above come from secondary searches (citations, literature reviews) and should be checked against the original texts if the precise efficiency gain (formula, magnitude) needs to be cited with certainty.
- No paper found that explicitly derives an estimator for "recovering an additive function s(x) under exact-quota censoring (top-K by position, not by probability)" with a proof of superiority (reduced variance) over the standard probit MLE. The theory of concomitants of order statistics and Type II censoring provide the correct probabilistic framework for describing the mechanism, but not (in what was found) an estimation/regression theory built on top of it.

## Are there results on rank regression, quota-based discrete choice models, or learning with "distributionally constrained labels"/"exact marginal constraints"?

### Takeaway
Yes: three relevant bodies of work exist — (1) calibration under known exact marginal constraints in machine learning (e.g. "rank-preserving calibration"), (2) the theory of optimal classification under a quota/cap (showing that the optimal rule is a threshold on the score), and (3) positive-unlabeled (PU) learning with a known class prior, which shares the same "P(y=1) known exactly" structure and offers concrete techniques (reweighting, post-processing, integration into the likelihood) transferable to the Zindi problem.

### Cited Findings
- Gaurav Sood, "Rank-preserving calibration for multiclass classification" (preprint) — adjusts predicted probabilities so that they match known class totals while preserving the model's internal ordering; formulates calibration as a Euclidean projection onto the intersection of a probability simplex and an isotonic-plus-sum set (Dykstra/ADMM solver, exact PAV pass + scalar shift for the column step). The paper argues that the classical prior correction (EM under label shift) can match the totals but "scramble" the ranking — [gsood.com PDF](https://gsood.com/research/papers/multiclass-rank-calibration.pdf)
- "Classification in Equilibrium: Structure of Optimal Decision Rules" (arXiv 2511.08347, 2025) — studies a cap on the fraction of individuals who can be "rewarded" (a quota); shows (Theorem 1) that the optimal classifier under this cap is a threshold (or negative-threshold) rule on the score — a theoretical justification for deciding by rank/quantile of the score rather than by a fixed probability threshold — [arXiv 2511.08347](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08347)
- Elkan & Noto (2008) and the PU literature that followed: a classifier can be validly learned in the positive-unlabeled setting **if the class prior (true P(y=1)) is known**; the true posterior probability can only be fully recovered from the "non-traditional posterior" if this prior is known — three families of techniques are identified: post-processing the outputs of a classifier trained on a surrogate label, reweighting examples before training, or integrating the prior directly into the learning algorithm — [secondary synthesis sourced from several PU papers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.01944), [ADAC 2021](https://home.ipipan.waw.pl/j.mielniczuk/ADAC_2021.pdf)
- Limitation noted in the PU literature: the Elkan-Noto prior estimator formally holds only for class-conditional distributions with disjoint supports, and a "partial matching" method tends to systematically overestimate the true class prior — an analytical and efficient penalized alternative has been proposed — [ADAC 2021](https://home.ipipan.waw.pl/j.mielniczuk/ADAC_2021.pdf)

### Inferences
- The most directly actionable principle for the Zindi competition, documented by several independent sources (Sood; PU learning), is: **do not settle for a probit MLE that lets the predicted positive rate float freely, but impose the exact "15% positives" constraint in post-processing**, via rank-preserving calibration (isotonic projection under a sum constraint) rather than a simple threshold recalibration. This could correct a residual calibration bias (distinct from a ranking bias) without touching the function s(x) itself — this is a gain of a different nature from the one discussed in the first question (which concerned the asymptotic efficiency of the estimator, not post-hoc calibration).
- The proof that the optimal decision rule under a quota is a threshold on the score (arXiv 2511.08347) validates the current practice (probit + threshold) after the fact, but says nothing about the optimal estimation of s(x) itself.

### Gaps
- No source found specifically addresses "rank regression" in the econometric sense combined with a known exact marginal constraint for a binary target — the connection with PU learning and calibration under total constraints is the best approximation found, not an exact match.
- No empirical comparison found (logloss or other metric) between a standard probit and a version "constrained to exactly 15%" on a similar problem — the magnitude of the practical gain remains speculative.

## Would semiparametric single-index estimators (Klein-Spady, Ichimura, Han MRC) be more efficient than a GBM/GAM for this specific mechanism?

### Takeaway
No, probably not — and this is an important conclusion that runs counter to the initial intuition: these estimators are designed to relax the assumption on the error distribution (unknown F) and/or the shape of the link, an assumption the user has already confirmed empirically (Gaussian, better than Student-t/GND). Cosslett (1987) formally shows that not knowing F **costs** efficiency compared with the correctly specified parametric case; Sherman (1993) shows that Han's rank estimator (MRC) does not even attain the semiparametric efficiency bound for binary choice. Using these methods here would amount to paying a robustness cost for an assumption (unknown link/shape of F) that does not need to be relaxed.

### Cited Findings
- Klein & Spady (1993, *Econometrica* 61(2), pp. 387-421), "An Efficient Semiparametric Estimator for Binary Response Models" — the index is **linear** (β'x), and it is the (unknown) link function that is estimated nonparametrically by kernel; the index coefficients are estimated by smoothed likelihood and, according to a secondary source, attain Chamberlain's semiparametric efficiency bound for this class of models — [metricgate.com](https://metricgate.com/calculator/klein-spady-binary-choice) (secondary source, to be verified in Econometrica)
- Ichimura (1993, *Journal of Econometrics*) — semiparametric least squares for single-index models, root-n-consistent and asymptotically normal for the coefficients up to a multiplicative constant; covers binary choice, censored/truncated Tobit, duration models — [reference cited in secondary search]
- Chen (2000) extends Klein-Spady: the intercept can be consistently estimated, but only under a conditional symmetry restriction on the error — [secondary reference via search]
- Sherman (1994) proves root-n-consistency and asymptotic normality of a generalized estimator that includes Ichimura and Klein-Spady as special cases — [secondary reference]
- Han (1987, *Journal of Econometrics* 35, pp. 303-316) introduces the maximum rank correlation (MRC) estimator, which maximizes a Kendall-type rank correlation between the outcome and the index, for models in which an unknown monotone transformation links the index to the observed outcome (binary choice, censored regression, proportional hazards as special cases) — [secondary summary sourced from Sherman 1993, Econometrica 61(1)]
- Sherman (1993, *Econometrica* 61(1), pp. 123-137) derives the limiting distribution of MRC: root-n-consistent and asymptotically normal, **but does not attain the semiparametric efficiency bound** for binary choice — [Cambridge/Econometric Society, The Limiting Distribution of the Maximum Rank Correlation Estimator](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/econometric-theory/article/maximum-rank-correlation-estimator-and-the-rank-estimator-in-binary-choice-models/362E4E43A54E35E55A564F26FF1D35A6)
- Han's estimator (MRC) requires a continuous regressor with a non-trivial effect on the index, and the parameters are only identified up to location — the intercept is excluded from consistent estimation — [secondary summary, academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/349536)
- Cosslett (1987): compared with the corresponding parametric efficiency bound, adaptive estimation (= no efficiency loss when F is ignored) is in general not possible for this model — [econometricsociety.org](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1987/05/01/efficiency-bounds-distribution-free-estimators-binary-choice)
- Chen (on symmetric binary choice): his semiparametric maximum likelihood estimator attains Cosslett's (1987) efficiency bound under symmetry and independence restrictions; Klein-Spady attains Chamberlain's (1986) bound under independence; imposing symmetry improves the efficiency of the slope parameters — [secondary summary via search on "efficient estimation of binary choice models under symmetry"]
- A 2025 IFN paper (Hjertstrand, Proctor, Westerlund) notes that the Klein-Spady estimator is known to be very difficult to implement in practice, with poor small-sample properties, and proposes a simplified version — [ifn.se PDF](https://ifn.se/media/1egafu2u/wp1535.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is a structural mismatch between these methods and the Zindi problem: Klein-Spady/Ichimura assume a **linear index** and an **unknown link** (the exact opposite of the user's situation, where the link/noise — Gaussian — is already confirmed, but where it is the **non-linear additive shape of s(x)** that is the object of estimation — a problem covered by GAMs/boosting, not by these linear single-index estimators).
- Han (MRC) would in principle be applicable (it requires no assumption on F or on the link), but Sherman (1993) shows that it is **less efficient** than methods that exploit the correct distributional information — so, since Gaussian noise has already been empirically confirmed as the best fit, using Han would amount to deliberately giving up exploitable information (the Gaussian form) to gain robustness that is not needed here.
- Honest conclusion: in this specific case (link/noise already correctly identified as Gaussian), the literature on semiparametric single-index models **does not support** the idea that they would do better than a well-specified standard probit likelihood on the shape s(x) — on the contrary, theory (Cosslett 1987; Sherman 1993) predicts that they would do worse, since they pay an efficiency cost to relax an assumption that has already been verified.

### Gaps
- No source found addresses the exact hybrid case of the Zindi problem: a **known** parametric link/noise (Gaussian) but a **nonparametric additive** index (neither linear nor of arbitrary free form) under quota censoring. The single-index methods (Klein-Spady, Ichimura, Han) all assume a linear index; their extension to a generalized additive index (GAM-index) combined with a known Gaussian link and quota censoring was not found in the literature consulted.
- The full text of Klein & Spady (1993) and Sherman (1993, 1994) was not consulted directly (only via secondary sources) — the claims about attained efficiency bounds should be verified against the original articles before being cited as certainties in a final report.

## Is there work on selection noise ("selection noise", "noisy sorting", "noisy top-k selection", noisy ranking) that is directly applicable?

### Takeaway
This literature exists but deals almost exclusively with recovering a ranking from **pairwise comparisons** (tournaments, crowdsourcing, Bradley-Terry/Mallows), without covariates/features or regression of a score function — it does not directly match the Zindi mechanism (a latent additive score as a function of covariates, perturbed by additive Gaussian noise, then censored by quota). Nothing directly applicable was found.

### Cited Findings
- Mao, Weed & Rigollet (ALT 2018, PMLR 83:821-847), "Minimax Rates and Efficient Algorithms for Noisy Sorting" — studies the noisy sorting model, a permutation-based pairwise comparison model; establishes optimal minimax rates under two sampling procedures and proposes a fast, near-optimal algorithm when observations are drawn independently — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1710.10388)
- Heckel, Shah, Ramchandran & Wainwright (2019, *Annals of Statistics*), "Active Ranking from Pairwise Comparisons and when Parametric Assumptions Don't Help" — active/sequential ranking of items from noisy pairwise comparisons, covering top-k identification and full ranking — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1606.08842)
- "Learning to Retrieve with Weakened Labels: Robust Training under Label Noise" (arXiv 2512.13237, Dec. 2025) — treats top-K selection as ambiguous supervision (a set of plausible candidates rather than a single relevant document), trains a top-K retriever via an optimistic "superset loss" — close in spirit (top-K selection under label ambiguity) but applied to information retrieval, not to regression of an additive score under Gaussian noise — [arXiv 2512.13237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13237)
- Literature on pairwise noise in learning to rank (LTR): a 2014 paper (*Information Retrieval Journal*) shows that it is the noise rate on document pairs (pNoise), rather than the per-document noise rate (dNoise), that best explains the performance degradation of a ranking algorithm — [springerprofessional.de](https://www.springerprofessional.de/doi/10.1145/2576230)
- PeerRank (IEEE Access, 2022) uses a "peer loss" to train LTR models on noisy labels without requiring clean data — [doaj.org](https://doaj.org/article/ff7a5b2f94a04fa8957965b37525b601)

### Inferences
- The "noisy sorting"/"noisy top-k" literature in ML is built around pairwise comparison queries without a continuous covariate structure — it answers "how to reconstruct a total order from noisy comparisons", not "how to estimate a function s(x) from covariates when only membership in the top 15% of the noisy score is observed". Direct transfer of these techniques (tournament-style algorithms, minimax bounds on the number of comparisons) to the Zindi regression problem seems limited; the theory of concomitants of order statistics (see first question) remains the closest probabilistic framework, but with no established link to this "noisy sorting" ML literature.

### Gaps
- No paper found explicitly combining "noisy top-K selection" and "regression of a function of covariates" (as opposed to pure ranking without features) — a clear gap in the literature consulted, to be flagged honestly to the author of the report: if such a method exists, it was not located in this search.
- It was not possible to check whether the techniques of the "noisy sorting" papers (Mao/Weed/Rigollet; Heckel et al.) have variants with covariates ("covariate-assisted noisy sorting" or equivalent) — further searching on this specific point could be useful but was not carried out (research budget reached).

## Overall conclusion (honesty on the central question)

### Takeaway
No method in the literature consulted constitutes a "turnkey", empirically validated solution specifically designed to "recover an additive score s(x) under exact top-K% quota censoring with confirmed Gaussian noise" and proven superior to a well-specified standard probit likelihood. The most firmly established result is in fact an argument **against** F-agnostic semiparametric methods (Klein-Spady, Ichimura, Han) in this specific case, since F (Gaussian) is already known/confirmed — and an argument **for** exploiting the exact margin constraint (15%) as an additional moment/calibration, but this documented gain (Cosslett, Imbens, Imbens & Lancaster) concerns a threshold/intercept parameter, not the shape of s(x), and its practical magnitude in this context is not quantified by the literature found.

### Cited Findings
(see sections above — no new source here, synthesis only)

### Inferences
- Actionable direction #1 (based on Cosslett/Imbens): explicitly impose, in estimation or in post-processing, that the predicted positive rate be exactly 15% (e.g. by fixing the decision threshold at the exact empirical 85th percentile of the fitted score rather than leaving a free intercept), which eliminates a source of variance on that specific parameter.
- Actionable direction #2 (based on Sood / calibration under exact constraints): apply a post-hoc calibration that preserves the model's ranking while forcing the sum of predicted probabilities to match the known quota exactly (isotonic projection under a sum constraint), rather than a simple classical Platt/isotonic correction that ignores this exact constraint.
- Direction to avoid (based on Cosslett 1987 / Sherman 1993): do not adopt semiparametric estimators that are agnostic to the noise distribution (Klein-Spady, Ichimura, Han MRC) — theory predicts they would be less, not more, efficient than the standard probit, since Gaussian noise is already confirmed as correctly specified.
- The most significant gap of this research: the theory of **concomitants of order statistics** / **Type II censoring** is, structurally, the closest and most faithful mathematical framework for the exact mechanism described (sorting, selecting the k largest values of a noisy score, covariates = concomitants) — but no regression estimation theory built specifically on this framework, with a proof of efficiency gain for estimating s(x), could be located. It is possible that such a theory simply does not yet exist in this precise form (additive regression under concomitant censoring), which would be consistent with the hypothesis that a well-specified standard probit remains, given the state of the statistical literature found, the most suitable method for the shape of s(x), even if the exact quota constraint may still bring a marginal gain via the threshold/calibration.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical comparison (simulation or theory) quantifying the exact variance gain of an estimate "constrained to exactly 15%" versus a free probit MLE, in a setting close to the user's (large n, multivariate additive score). Without this quantification, it is impossible to say whether actionable direction #1/#2 above would produce a logloss gain large enough to close the gap between 0.22786 and the 0.223771 target.
