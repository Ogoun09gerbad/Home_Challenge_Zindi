## Which Kaggle/Zindi/DrivenData competitions (2022-2026) have a known generator based on score + Gaussian noise + quota selection?

### Takeaway
No source found publicly confirms, for any Kaggle/Zindi/DrivenData competition, a documented generating mechanism identical to yours (additive hidden score + independent Gaussian noise + selection of the top 15% as positives). The closest documented synthetic cases (Kaggle "Instant Gratification", "Don't Overfit II", Tabular Playground Series, Santander Customer Transaction Prediction) have synthetic generators that are *known or strongly suspected*, but rely on other mechanisms (multi-cluster Gaussian mixtures, CTGAN/GAN, or duplicated/augmented data) — not on an explicit probit/quota threshold model. The competition that structurally best matches your case appears to be your own competition: the research identified its likely name on Zindi (see below), but without public confirmation of the generation mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- The "Kaggle Book" chapter (Packt) states that the monthly "Tabular Playground Series" competitions (TPS, launched in 2021) used synthetic datasets generated by a CTGAN-type network to replicate public data or past competitions — not a score+noise+quota mechanism. — [Kaggle Book ch.9](https://packt-plus-non-live.prod.packtpub.com/en-at/product/the-kaggle-book-9781801817479/chapter/modeling-for-tabular-competitions-9/section/the-tabular-playground-series-ch09lvl1sec47)
- For the Kaggle "Instant Gratification" competition (2019), a 6th-place solution documents that the underlying synthetic data was structured per class as Gaussian clusters (a mixture of 2-3 clusters per class), and that exploiting this known structure (clustering + GMM/QDA, standardization to stabilize the covariance matrices, regularization) improved validation AUC compared with a black-box approach. — [Kaggle discussion, 6th place write-up](https://kaggle.com/c/mayo-clinic-strip-ai/discussion/358029) (link found via search; content of the Instant Gratification write-up cited from the search summary)
- Direct search: a competition named **"AI4EAC Liquidity Stress Early Warning Challenge"** was identified on Zindi (zindi.africa / zindi.world), whose description closely matches your challenge: target = the customer will experience "liquidity stress" within 30 days of the observation date, restricted to students from 8 East/Central African countries, total prize 7,500 USD. The combined scoring formula is **Log Loss weighted 60% + ROC-AUC weighted 40%**, which exactly matches your formula `0.4*AUC + 0.6*(1-logloss/0.595)`. — [Zindi, competition page](https://zindi.africa/competitions/liquidity-stress-early-warning-challenge), [data page](https://zindi.world/competitions/liquidity-stress-early-warning-challenge/data)
- The "data" page of this competition (viewed via cache) specifies neither a generation mechanism, nor an explicitly synthetic nature, nor class imbalance, nor a scoring formula — this information is not publicly documented on the main page (possibly present in `data_dictionary.csv` or the starter notebook, which were not consulted). — [Zindi, data page](https://zindi.world/competitions/liquidity-stress-early-warning-challenge/data)
- No mention found of a 1st-place TPS write-up with logloss documenting a score+noise+quota mechanism. — multiple searches with no conclusive result.

### Inferences
- It is very likely that the identified competition ("AI4EAC Liquidity Stress Early Warning Challenge") is exactly the one in the user's context (`liquidity_stress_next_30d`), given the exact match of the scoring formula (0.6 logloss / 0.4 AUC) and of the target definition. This means Zindi's public documentation itself neither confirms nor refutes the score+noise+quota mechanism — this knowledge remains an empirical inference specific to your team, neither corroborated (nor contradicted) by an external source.
- The fact that none of the major publicly documented synthetic generators (TPS/CTGAN, Instant Gratification/GMM, Don't Overfit II) exactly matches an "additive score + independent Gaussian noise + top-N% quota" model suggests that this design, although statistically classical (probit/quota model), is not a standard pattern widely documented in public competition write-ups — or else that participants rarely recognize it explicitly as such, even when it is present.

### Gaps

## What do winners' write-ups say about the techniques that made the difference in the last 0.5-1% of score?

### Takeaway
The sources found do not document precise score-gain figures for the last 0.5-1% in a strictly comparable context (score+noise+quota). The most concrete precedent found (Instant Gratification, 6th place) shows a qualitative gain tied to exploiting the known structure of the generator (Gaussian clustering) rather than to generic techniques (boosting, blending), but without an exact numerical delta reported in the retrieved excerpts.

### Cited Findings
- On Instant Gratification, the author of the 6th-place write-up reports running about 1000 experiments on their own synthetic data reproducing the generator, and finding that using 3 clusters per class (instead of 2) gave better validation and test AUC; most of the gain came from reproducing/exploiting the generator's structure rather than from the choice of algorithm (GMM retained after testing alternatives). — [Kaggle discussion, summary retrieved via search]
- The academic paper "Climbing the Kaggle Leaderboard by Exploiting the Log-Loss Oracle" (Whitehill, arXiv:1707.01825) shows that by repeatedly querying a public log-loss oracle, it is possible to reconstruct the test labels and reach a log-loss of 0.00000 in a competition — i.e. a "perfect" score obtained not by a model but by exploiting the leaderboard mechanism itself. The paper stresses that log-loss, which can be computed per individual example (unlike AUC, which depends on pairs), is particularly vulnerable to this kind of exploitation. — [arXiv 1707.01825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01825)

### Inferences
- In a setting where the generator is known/suspected (score+noise+quota), the marginal gain documented in the competition literature typically comes from explicitly modeling the statistical structure of the noise rather than from stacking generic techniques — which is consistent with your own strategy (GAM with a probit link). However, no source makes it possible to quantify whether a gap of 0.003-0.005 in composite score (hence roughly 0.002-0.005 in logloss) is "within reach of good techniques" or "out of reach without additional information" — this question remains without a quantified empirical answer in the available sources.
- The log-loss oracle precedent (Whitehill) is a relevant methodological caveat: an abnormally low and stable logloss score can, in some leaderboard designs, result from exploiting the scoring mechanism rather than from a genuinely better model. But this specific mechanism (repeated probing of a public oracle) a priori only applies to metrics computable by querying the public leaderboard repeatedly and in a targeted way — most modern platforms (Zindi included) limit the number of daily submissions precisely to prevent this type of attack.

### Gaps
- No competition write-up found reports a precise numerical delta in logloss/AUC/composite score corresponding to the "last 0.5-1%" in a score+noise+quota context strictly analogous to yours.
- No source specifically covers fold-wise pseudo-labeling, deep GAM/GBM stacking, or feature engineering targeted at the noise structure in a documented quota-based competition context — this part of the question remains without a sourced answer.

## Is there public documentation on the "rank 1 with a high score from the very first submission" phenomenon and its typical explanations (cheating, multiple accounts, luck, skill)?

### Takeaway
Yes: there is solid public documentation on 1st-place cheating cases on Kaggle (the detailed PetFinder.my 2020 case) and on the enforcement of anti-multi-account rules on Zindi (several cases 2020-2026, with quantified penalties), but no source specifically addresses the precise sub-case "score unchanged since the very first submission" — the signal investigators use in practice is an abnormal gap between 1st and 2nd place, and the sub-case is not documented as a named pattern.

### Cited Findings
- **Kaggle PetFinder.my case (revealed January 2020):** The winning team "Bestpetting", led by Kaggle Grandmaster Pavel Pleskov, scraped thousands of PetFinder.my profiles with their actual adoption times, and stored them as hashes hidden among Pixabay data in a function named `majority_voting`. About 3,500 scraped profiles matched the ~4,000 profiles in the test set. To avoid suspicion, the lookup was only used ~10% of the time. Prize at stake: 25,000 USD in total, 10,000 USD for 1st place. ~2000 teams, more than 3000 submissions. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2020/01/21/ai_kaggle_contest_cheat/)
- **Discovery of the PetFinder.my case:** Benjamin Minixhofer (19, Austria, ranked 6th), while helping the organizer combine the methods of the top teams, noticed an abnormally large score gap between 1st and 2nd place, examined the winning code, and found the hidden lookup. He estimated that without cheating, Bestpetting would have ranked around 100th. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2020/01/21/ai_kaggle_contest_cheat/)
- **PetFinder.my consequences:** Kaggle deleted Pleskov's account and banned him from the platform; the team was disqualified and lost the 10,000 USD 1st prize; Pleskov was fired from H2O.ai (where he had worked for ~4 months) after about 4 months. — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2020/01/21/ai_kaggle_contest_cheat/)
- **Zindi, TAHMO Solar Radiation case (May 2026):** Participants reported a group of accounts created on the same day with a pattern suggesting duplicated activity. Zindi confirmed it had removed these accounts (and others) from the leaderboard and placed them on probation; one commenter objected that only the secondary accounts had been removed, not the main accounts. — [Zindi discussion #33081](https://zindi.africa/competitions/tahmo-incoming-solar-radiation-prediction-challenge/discussions/33081), [#33202](https://zindi.africa/competitions/tahmo-incoming-solar-radiation-prediction-challenge/discussions/33202)
- **Zindi announcement (May 20, 2026):** Zindi announced that its investigation led to sanctions against several users for rule violations (prohibited coordinated activity, sharing of submissions), while stating it could not publicly comment on individual accounts/investigations. — [Zindi blog](https://zindi.africa/blog/announcement-important-updates-to-zindis-rules-and-submission-guidelines)
- **Zindi rules on multiple accounts:** Having multiple accounts, collaborating, or being a member of multiple teams in the same competition is prohibited. Penalties: 1st offense = no prizes for 6 months + removal of 2000 points from the profile; 2nd offense = permanent ban from the platform. — [Zindi discussion](https://zindi.africa/discussions/15064) / [competition](https://zindi.africa/competitions/zindi-new-user-engagement-prediction-challenge/discussions/15064)
- **Historical statement by Zindi's CEO (2020):** Since 2019, Zindi has disqualified 36 individuals from competitions and banned 4 others from the platform. — (cited in the search results, Zindi 2020 source, not verified directly in a primary document for this note)
- **Zindi case, row duplication (2023):** A thread reports that the leaderboard computation counted duplicated rows in the submission, allowing some entries to obtain an abnormally high score — an artifact of the scoring mechanism rather than confirmed intentional cheating. — [search results, not re-verified by direct fetch]
- **Zindi case, Luganda ASR:** A commenter noted that the 1st place on the public leaderboard (identical/abnormal score) ended up only 7th on the final winners' leaderboard, which they interpreted as a sign of train/test leakage rather than genuine skill — illustrating that the gap between the public leaderboard and the final winners' ranking is a commonly used signal for detecting such anomalies. — [Zindi discussion #10101](https://zindi.africa/competitions/mozilla-luganda-automatic-speech-recognition/discussions/10101)
- **Academic work on leaderboard integrity:** The paper "Climbing the Kaggle Leaderboard by Exploiting the Log-Loss Oracle" explicitly stresses the importance of "commonsense safeguards" to preserve ranking integrity against this kind of score-oracle exploitation. — [arXiv 1707.01825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01825)

### Inferences
- Platforms and the community consistently use the same signal to detect a ranking anomaly: an abnormally large score gap between rank 1 and rank 2 (PetFinder.my case), or a divergence between the public leaderboard and the private/final leaderboard (Luganda ASR case). A rank-1 score that has remained stable since the very first submission is not, in itself, documented as a cheating signal in the sources found — it may just as well result from genuine immediate skill (an already near-optimal solution submitted from the start) as from data leakage or access to external information. Without an abnormal quantified gap relative to rank 2, and without an observable public/private divergence before the close, the available sources do not allow choosing between these hypotheses for your case.
- Zindi has an active and recent track record (2020, 2023, May 2026) of detecting and sanctioning anomalous leaderboard behavior, which suggests that a well-argued report (score gap, suspicious patterns) has a good chance of being taken seriously if you decide to submit it to Zindi support — but this does not constitute evidence that the rank-1 case in your competition falls into this type of behavior.

### Gaps
- No source specifically documents the sub-pattern "rank-1 score identical since the first submission" as a named or studied signal in its own right (neither on Kaggle, Zindi, nor Reddit r/MachineLearning) — the searches performed (including in English, with varied phrasings) returned no relevant results on this precise point.
- No confirmation or refutation specific to rank 1 of the AI4EAC Liquidity Stress Early Warning Challenge (or of `liquidity_stress_next_30d`) could be obtained — no indexed Zindi discussion was found for this specific competition concerning its leaderboard or any report.
- The Zindi CEO's statement about "36 disqualified / 4 banned since 2019" comes from a search-results summary and has not been verified by directly consulting the primary source (original article/press release) — to be treated with caution until the primary source is confirmed.
