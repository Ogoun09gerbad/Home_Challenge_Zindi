# Statistical theory of exact-quota selection on an additive score + Gaussian noise (Tobit/Heckman/censored probit type, noisy top-k)

## Question 1 — Is there a known theoretical bound (Cramér-Rao or direct computation) on the logloss/AUC loss caused by selection noise, as a function of SNR?

### Takeaway
No published "off-the-shelf" bound handles exactly our mechanism (exact 15% quota on an additive score + Gaussian noise, followed by supervised learning on the resulting labels). However, signal detection theory (the "binormal" model / parametric ROC) gives an **exact** closed-form formula linking the Bayes-optimal AUC to the signal-to-noise ratio. It is the closest and most directly usable quantified result the research found, but it answers a slightly different question from yours (achievable AUC as a function of the generator's SNR, not the "loss due to noise" in absolute terms compared with a noiseless case).

### Cited Findings
- In the equal-variance binormal model (negatives ~ N(0,1), positives ~ N(a,1)), the Bayes-optimal AUC has the closed form **AUC = Φ(a/√2)**, equivalently **a = √2·Φ⁻¹(AUC)**, where `a` is the standardized mean separation (from the distribution of the difference of two Gaussian variables, with mean a and variance 2) — [MetricGate, Parametric ROC / signal detection](https://metricgate.com/docs/parametric-roc-signal-detection/).
- In the general binormal case (unequal variances), the formula generalizes to **AUC = Φ(a/√(1+b²))**, with a = (μ₁−μ₀)/σ₁ and b = σ₀/σ₁; the historical reference cited is Metz & Kronman (1980) — [MetricGate, Metz binormal ROC fit](https://metricgate.com/docs/metz-binormal-roc-fit/).
- For logarithmic loss (logloss), the minimal (Bayes) expected loss when the predictive model is exactly calibrated to the true distribution equals the entropy of the true distribution; any loss beyond that entropy is a KL divergence between the predictor and the true distribution: E[logloss] = H(D) + KL(D‖q̂), the achievable minimum being H(D), independent of q̂ — [general source on the entropy/KL decomposition of logloss](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.00252).
- A recent paper dedicated exactly to estimating the "Bayes-optimal BER and AUC" exists (Ushio, Ishida, Sugiyama, arXiv 2609.02304), but its detailed content (exact Bayes BER/AUC formulas under Gaussian noise, SNR→AUC numerical tables) could not be extracted: the PDF was not usable by the retrieval tool and the HTML version (arxiv.org/html/2609.02304v1) returns a 404 error — only the title and authors are confirmed.
- No Cramér-Rao-type bound for *binary classification* under Gaussian noise was found. The available Cramér-Rao results concern estimating a signal parameter from 1-bit quantized binary measurements (CRB for a binary sensor network, or for quantized data), not the logloss/AUC loss of a classifier — [CRB for binary sensor networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.7436); [CRB for quantized data](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13182). The latter notes an interesting but not directly relevant numerical result: for a binary ADC with an optimal time-varying threshold, the resulting CRB is only **π/2 times larger** than the standard (unquantized) CRB — an order of magnitude for the degradation due to binarization, not to selection noise per se.
- The literature on noisy top-k selection ("noisy top-k selection", noisy comparisons) gives sample/comparison complexity bounds (e.g. Θ(n² log n) comparisons for one-round noisy sorting; Braverman, Mao, Peres, COLT 2019), but these are bounds on the number of comparisons needed to recover an ordering, not bounds on predictive logloss/AUC loss — [Sorted Top-K in Rounds](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v99/braverman19a.html).
- A recent preprint on identifying the top-K in noisy networks gives a bound of the form **min{λ/m, σ²/(m−λ)²}** on the probability of misclassifying a node in the top-K under noise, but it is a network setting (not an additive score + quota selection used to generate ML labels), and it is a very recent preprint not yet validated by the community — [arXiv 2605.24734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24734).
- The literature on selection/ranking as "compound decisions" (ranking and selection) shows qualitatively that at low SNR, selecting the top N% yields a high rate of "false positives of merit" (true high performers cannot be distinguished from lucky ones), and that this rate decreases as the dispersion of true effects grows — but without a numerical formula directly transferable to our AUC/logloss — [Invidious Comparisons: Ranking and Selection as Compound Decisions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.12550).

### Inferences
- Applying the binormal formula above (AUC = Φ(a/√2)) to the displayed rank 1 (AUC = 0.925214) gives an implied standardized separation a = √2·Φ⁻¹(0.925214) ≈ 2.04. **This is a direct computation made by the researcher from the cited formula, not a published result**: it should be treated as an illustration of the method, not as a "finding" from the literature. It also assumes that the best model's score follows an equal-variance binormal model, an assumption not verified here.
- The fact that the binormal formula is closed-form and exact suggests an operational lead: if you know (or estimate) the generator's Var(noise)/Var(signal) ratio, you can compute the exact theoretical Bayes-optimal AUC for comparison with rank 1 — but no source found performs this computation for an *exact quota* mechanism (selecting the top N%) rather than a simple fixed threshold on score+noise; exact-quota selection on a finite sample introduces an additional dependence (the threshold becomes random, a function of the sample) that none of the sources found address.

### Gaps
- No source provides a numerical bound directly comparable to the observed gap of 0.003–0.005 in composite score (~0.002–0.005 in logloss). It is not possible to say whether this gap is "within the expected order of magnitude" of selection noise or whether it reflects real room for model improvement — no literature settles this question for this specific mechanism.
- The exact content of the dedicated "Bayes-Optimal BER and AUC" paper (arXiv 2609.02304) could not be consulted (PDF extraction failure, 404 on the HTML version). It may contain formulas or numerical tables directly relevant to Q1 — to be re-checked manually if possible (try `https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02304` directly in a browser, or another version of the PDF).
- No Cramér-Rao-type bound was found specifically for "logloss/AUC loss due to quota selection" — the question remains without a formal quantified answer in the accessible literature.

## Question 2 — Which concrete techniques (formulas, not just names) make it possible to get closer to the theoretical bound beyond the standard probit likelihood?

### Takeaway
The literature gives concrete formulas for correcting attenuation caused by a mismeasured latent variable (classical attenuation bias, correction via the reliability ratio) and for deconvolution of Gaussian noise with known vs unknown variance, but no source proposes a technique "beyond probit" specifically designed for our quota-selection mechanism; the techniques found are generic (errors-in-variables, Heckman/Tobit, deconvolution) and need to be adapted.

### Cited Findings
- **Classical attenuation factor (linear case)**: if the observed predictor is the true signal plus an independent classical error, the estimated regression coefficient is biased by a factor equal to the **reliability ratio σ²_x/(σ²_x+σ²_u)**; dividing the naive slope by this ratio gives the correction, provided σ²_u is known (or estimated) — [MetricGate, attenuation correction](https://metricgate.com/docs/attenuation-bias-correction/).
- **Spearman's formula (correlation attenuation)**: the observed correlation equals the true correlation divided by the square root of the product of the two reliabilities; an observed correlation cannot exceed the square root of the product of the two reliabilities (ceiling on observable correlation) — [AttenuationOfCorrelations, McGill Biostat](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/Surveys/AttenuationOfCorrelations-2016-Teaching_Statistics.pdf). Noted limitation: the formula can produce corrected correlations >1.00 when reliabilities are poorly estimated — [arXiv 1911.01576](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01576).
- **Probit correction for measurement error (specific case found)**: a 2026 paper on calibrating local items derives an expansion factor to invert the attenuation due to the probit convolution: factor = (1 − â²σ̂²_post/D²)^(−1/2), the corrected slope being this factor multiplied by the uncorrected slope — formula specific to its context (test calibration), transferability to our case not demonstrated — [arXiv 2608.11542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11542).
- **Deconvolution with known vs unknown Gaussian noise variance**: Matias (2002) shows that when the noise is centered Gaussian but of **unknown variance**, the convergence rate of the signal density estimate degrades seriously compared with the known-variance case, and that this degradation propagates to nonlinear errors-in-variables models — direct implication: **knowing/precisely estimating the generator's noise σ (if possible) pays off more than merely specifying the probit link** — [Semiparametric deconvolution with unknown noise variance, Matias 2002](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.1051/ps:2002015/).
- **Heckman/probit selection model**: the variance of the selection equation's error term is not identified and is normalized to 1; bias appears if the correlation between the errors of the outcome and selection equations is not controlled for — a useful but qualitative standard reminder — [UH lecture notes on selection](https://www.uh.edu/~bsorense/selection.pdf).
- **Identification of ρ in Heckman**: without an exclusion restriction, identification of ρ relies solely on the nonlinearity of the inverse Mills ratio, which creates a risk of severe multicollinearity — relevant if you were considering adding a Mills-ratio-type term to your pipeline — [metricgate.com, Heckman selection model](https://metricgate.com/docs/heckman-selection-model).
- **EM for Tobit (estimating the noise variance from the data)**: the E step imputes the expected latent value via the inverse Mills ratio (E[y*|y=c] = xᵀβ − σλ(z), λ(z)=φ(z)/Φ(z)); the M step re-estimates β and σ by OLS on the augmented data with a variance adjustment for censored observations — a concrete mechanism for **estimating the noise σ directly from the data**, potentially applicable to your case to refine σ̂ beyond a simple probit maximum-likelihood estimate — [MetricGate, Bayesian Tobit](https://metricgate.com/docs/bayesian-tobit-regression/).
- **AUC under label noise (correction formula)**: Charoenphakdee et al. show that the relationship between the AUC risk computed on noisy labels and the AUC risk on clean labels can be expressed in closed form — referenced in a paper on "symmetric losses" for robust learning — [arXiv 2101.01366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.01366). The details of the formula could not be extracted from the search snippet.

### Inferences
- The two most concrete and actionable levers identified are: (a) precise estimation of σ (noise variance) from the data via a Tobit-style EM scheme rather than a simple probit fit (which could reduce the "unknown variance" type of loss documented by Matias 2002), and (b) an errors-in-variables-style attenuation correction if your score s_hat(x) is itself a noisy estimate of the true latent s(x) (reliability ratio applied as post-processing to the predicted probabilities, or to the latent score before applying the Φ link).
- None of these techniques has a published numerical guarantee regarding the logloss/AUC gain it would deliver in an exact-quota selection setting (as opposed to a fixed threshold) — extrapolating the expected gain to your 0.003-0.005 gap is not supported by any source.

### Gaps
- No technique found is specifically designed for the "exact quota selection" mechanism (as opposed to a fixed/random selection threshold independent of the sample): quota selection introduces a dependence between the labels of all individuals (each label depends on the relative ranking of all the others), which none of the classical Tobit/Heckman sources address (they assume a fixed threshold, not an empirical quantile).
- No formula was found that explicitly combines "errors-in-variables correction" + "probit link" + "quota selection" in a single unified theoretical framework.

## Question 3 — Are there published numerical results (documented logloss/AUC gains) comparable in size to our 0.003-0.005 gap?

### Takeaway
No: no source found documents a numerical logloss or AUC gain, in a context of synthetic label generation via quota+noise in an ML competition (Kaggle/Zindi/DrivenData), that is comparable in magnitude (0.003-0.005) to your gap. The research did not identify any paper or write-up explicitly covering this type of synthetic generator with a discussion of a performance ceiling.

### Cited Findings
- No Kaggle or Zindi competition found in the search results explicitly documents a "quota + Gaussian noise" mechanism with a discussion of a theoretical performance ceiling. The results obtained concern adjacent but non-equivalent topics:
  - A Zindi competition ("Don't Overfit") uses AUC as its metric and forbids rounding/thresholding the submitted probabilities to prevent cheating via a score "oracle" — but does not involve quota+noise generation — [Zindi, Don't Overfit](https://zindi.africa/competitions/dont-overfit-by-uofk-fmsi).
  - Work on exploiting a logloss/AUC "oracle" in Kaggle competitions shows that test labels can be reconstructed from the returned loss scores, but this concerns information leakage through the metric, not a theoretical ceiling due to generator noise — [Reconstructing Test Labels from Noisy Loss Functions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03022); [Whitehill, AAAI workshop, logloss oracle on Kaggle](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/ws/ws0421/16161-75972-1-PB.pdf) (the latter reports a logloss of 0.00000 obtained by exploiting the oracle, rank #4/848 — an anecdote about exploiting a loophole, not a selection-noise result).
- A "noise ceiling" literature exists in the context of LLM-judged benchmarks (not synthetic tabular ML): a recent paper estimates reliability asymptotes between 0.600 and 0.792 for various benchmarks judged by a single LLM — a distant analogy, a completely different domain, not numerically transferable — [arXiv 2609.27787](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27787).

### Inferences
- The absence of results does not mean the 0.003-0.005 gap is abnormal or normal — simply that no published comparative data exist to settle the question. Given the binormal formula (Q1), a gap of this size in AUC would correspond to a very small shift in the standardized separation parameter a (on the order of a few thousandths on Φ⁻¹), which is consistent with the idea that the final ranking gaps in this kind of competition stem more from the fine-grained estimation variance of the model (hyperparameter choices, calibration) than from an unmodeled selection-noise phenomenon — but this remains a **researcher's extrapolation**, not a sourced result.

### Gaps
- Complete absence of any published, quantified numerical result for a quota+Gaussian-noise label generation mechanism in a synthetic ML competition context. No write-up was found on Kaggle, Zindi, or DrivenData explicitly addressing this topic.
- It was not possible to determine whether the 0.003-0.005 gap is "within the expected order of magnitude" of selection noise or whether it represents real, achievable room for improvement — this question remains open and should be explored empirically (Monte Carlo simulation of the generator with the known σ and quota, compared with the theoretical Bayes-optimal performance computable directly, rather than through further literature search).

## General methodological note (covering all 3 questions)
The research used ~17 web search queries and 2 page-retrieval attempts (including 1 PDF extraction failure and 1 404 error). The main finding, to be reported honestly to the author of the final report: **the academic literature does not directly address the exact mechanism "additive score + Gaussian noise + exact quota selection to generate a binary label in an ML competition context"**. The three most relevant adjacent bodies of literature are (1) signal detection theory / binormal ROC (exact formula AUC=Φ(a/√2)), (2) Tobit/Heckman/censored probit models (EM to estimate σ, Mills ratio), and (3) errors-in-variables theory (attenuation, Spearman, deconvolution with known vs unknown variance). None of these bodies provides a bound or numerical result directly comparable to the targeted 0.003-0.005 gap. Any estimate of this ceiling for your specific case would require a direct derivation/simulation (Monte Carlo with known σ, quota, and distribution of s(x)) rather than further bibliographic research.
